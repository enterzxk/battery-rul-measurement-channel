## Abstract

Battery health estimation depends on feature references as well as learners. Changing one column’s normalization moves root-mean-square error (RMSE) by up to 108%. We construct a self-referenced lift (SRL) from each cell’s earliest retained record. Under the offline author protocol, learners with the full history stack improve on 9 of 11 units after Holm correction, with none significantly worse and two units per backbone not significantly different. A companion benchmark with implicit self-comparison improves in 6 of 6 batches with per-batch selection (5 of 6 with a fixed configuration); an unseen-library legacy diagnostic improves by 22–33%. Deployability concerns feature computability, not a strict-online accuracy advantage.

## 1. Introduction

Battery energy storage is central to sustainable power systems: it absorbs renewable variability, provides frequency and capacity services, and extends the life of costly, carbon-intensive assets. All these functions depend on knowing how healthy each cell is. State-of-health (SOH) estimation is thus not merely a diagnostic convenience but an operational and economic requirement for battery management systems (BMS) in grid-scale storage and electrified transport: accurate estimates enable deeper usable state-of-charge windows, better-timed maintenance, and defensible second-life decisions, reducing lifetime cost and material footprint.

Data-driven SOH estimation has advanced rapidly: a prominent line embeds empirical degradation equations into neural networks as soft constraints, establishing the four processed libraries, official cell-level splits, and per-experiment results used here [1], while a companion benchmark supplies five deep baselines under three input types [2]. It has since grown through 2025–2026 with physics-informed variants [3,4,5], physics-embedded transfer learning [6], few-shot cross-domain frameworks [7], and quantum-feature hybrids [8]. A smaller parallel literature studies features, not architectures: a single interpretable quantity from early-cycle discharge curves predicts cycle life with a linear model [9]; cycle-difference features populate open battery machine-learning platforms [10]; recent work pairs interpretable feature fragments with spatio-temporal learners [11]; and in the wider time-series and tabular literature, simple transforms with standard learners form an established genre [12,13]. The closest of these, the early-cycle discharge-curve difference of [9], is also a within-cell comparison: a single hand-designed quantity, read from a fixed early window, predicting end of life. Here, however, the reference itself is the object of study: which past state each cycle is compared against, whether the pairing is responsible for the accuracy it buys, and whether it survives being computable in service. A third development is tabular foundation models, which infer training-free on small tabular tasks [14] yet require explicit time-derived features for temporal problems [15]; two recent efforts bring them into battery health—one distilling a time-series model [16], one coupling a tabular model to a physics-informed network by residual correction [17], and both are training-based hybrids—while large multi-source battery models begin to appear [18]. Retrieval-based context construction for these models works on i.i.d. benchmarks [19,20].

Nearly all of this work rests on a rarely examined assumption: the representation fed to the model—per-cycle statistical features under standard normalization—is settled preprocessing. The motivation is a feature-reference question, not an identity imposed by the label: the benchmark SOH is present discharge capacity divided by nominal capacity, not the measured first-cycle capacity. Comparing each unit against its own baseline is standard practice in other longitudinal settings: change-from-baseline and ANCOVA analyses in clinical trials [21], within (fixed-effect) transformations in panel econometrics [22], and explicit handling of unknown initial wear in prognostics datasets [23]. Battery pipelines are implicitly consistent with this: normalizing each cell by its own full-lifetime extrema compares every cell against itself. That convention suits offline assessment with complete trajectories, but cannot be reproduced in service, because cells differ widely in lifetime and a cell in operation does not know its own lifetime extrema. Total life is not the cycle-level SOH target studied here; nevertheless, the offline-helpful comparison is unavailable online.

This paper makes the comparison explicit, minimal, and deployment-computable, then tests—with a randomized mispairing control rather than a causal identification argument—whether the pairing itself is responsible for the observed gains. Our contributions are as follows.

*   We identify and quantify what features are compared against as a first-order design variable in degradation feature engineering, hidden inside routine normalization choices, and show its effect spans

*   We define the self-referenced lift (SRL), an explicit, deployment-computable, model-agnostic feature family, and separate its computability from its accuracy. The offline author protocol yields Holm-significant improvements over a physics-informed network on 9 of 11 units for each of two backbones, including learner effects; the corrected strict online evaluation does not reproduce an overall accuracy gain from adding compact SRL.

*   We introduce a randomization-based validation protocol for representation claims—randomized mispairing, affine negative controls, permutation inference, and pre-registration [24,25,26,27]—and use it to show that self-pairing, not generic trend information, is the active ingredient. Because the control manipulates the feature matrix and not the ageing process, this is evidence about the representation, not an interventional claim about the cell ([Section 2.6](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec2dot6-energies-19-04805)).

*   We describe a model- and protocol-specific carrier pattern consistent with HUST and TJU-3, with counterexamples on other units, and give the operating conditions for the method. The attribution is measured for the tree model under the author-protocol normalization; the foundation-model probe of [Section 3.10](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot10-energies-19-04805) lacks that structure, so we present it as a model- and protocol-scoped account, not as a property of the data.

## 2. Materials and Methods

For readers coming from an electrochemistry background, the machine-learning vocabulary used below is fixed as follows. Cross-cell estimation means that the model is fitted on cells whose full trajectories are available and tested on cells it has never seen; the latter are the held-out cells of an official split, reserved by the dataset authors. Primary-benchmark settings are not selected on them; the companion best-of-four summary is explicitly a post hoc choice using held-out scores and is separated from its fixed configuration ([Section 3.5](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot5-energies-19-04805)). An evaluation unit is the group of cells over which results are averaged, here one ageing batch or one library. A feature family is a set of columns obtained from one and the same comparison; an ablation removes one family at a time to measure what it contributes. Permutation importance measures how much error rises when a column’s values are shuffled, and a permutation test asks whether an observed difference survives relabelling. A derangement is a relabelling with no element left in place, and a negative control is an arm whose true effect is known to be zero, so that it can only reveal artefacts of the measurement itself.

### 2.1. Datasets and Splits

The primary benchmark consists of the four processed libraries released with the reference physics-informed work [1]: XJTU (six ageing batches), TJU (three batches) [28], HUST [29], and MIT [9], giving 11 evaluation units in total (387 cells, 82 of them held out); the MIT unit pools the three original sub-batches of that release (12 May 2017, 30 June 2017 and 12 April 2018) into a single unit, so it is not broken out per sub-batch as the XJTU batches are. The libraries span three cathode chemistries (NCM, NCA and LFP), with one batch that mixes two of them, and nominal capacities from 1.1 to 3.5 Ah; [Appendix A](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#app1-energies-19-04805) lists chemistry, nominal capacity, cell counts, per-cell cycle-count ranges and the lowest observed SOH for every unit. We adopt the official cell-level splits without modification; held-out cells are never used for fitting or model selection; normalization statistics follow the pipeline and are stated per pipeline below. In particular, Pipeline A scales each cell by its own full-lifetime extrema, which is same-cell future information that a cell in service cannot know; we treat that convention as offline-only and never pool its numbers with Pipeline B. The secondary benchmark is the companion deep-learning benchmark [2] on XJTU (55 cells, 67 handcrafted features, leave-one-cell-out splits). For external verification, we use an independent library, MATR-CLO (45 cells), the closed-loop-optimisation batch of Attia et al. [30]. It is disjoint from the three MIT batches in the primary benchmark and from the MATR batch used during development, and it took part in no design or selection decision; every use of it was preceded by criteria frozen in the corresponding experiment script.

Cell counts per evaluation unit are fixed by the official protocol and range from two or three held-out cells (each XJTU batch, TJU-3) to 20–23 held-out cells (HUST, MIT); this constrains per-unit statistical power and is treated explicitly in [Section 4.5](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec4dot5-energies-19-04805).

### 2.3. Two Evaluation Pipelines

Because the central issue is what can be computed in service, all experiments are run in one of two clearly separated pipelines, and numbers from the two are never pooled.

Pipeline A (reference convention, offline). Normalization follows the reference protocol: each cell is min–max scaled by its own full-lifetime extrema, and the cycle column is min–max scaled by the cell’s full retained length. Its author-offline reader also filters rows using full-lifetime statistics including the capacity label ([Section 3.7](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot7-energies-19-04805)). This pipeline is used for like-for-like comparison against published results, since both sides then operate under identical rules.

Pipeline B (deployment-computable). Normalization statistics are fitted on source cells only and applied unchanged to held-out cells; the cycle column is scaled by a fixed constant (

) so that it does not depend on total life; all other features remain history-only. Each Pipeline B input is computable once the current cycle’s required charge segment has completed; this is cycle-level availability, not continuous estimation during a partial cycle.

### 2.5. Metrics and Effect-Size Reference

The primary metric is root-mean-square error (RMSE) computed per held-out cell and then averaged across cells, which we verified to be the metric implemented in the reference work’s own scoring code. Published opponent values are taken from the officially released per-experiment results (ten repetitions per unit); we additionally executed the reference training code in our environment on one representative unit (XJTU-2C) and confirmed that the released values are consistent with code behaviour (ten-run distributions statistically indistinguishable, Mann–Whitney

; our rerun mean is 9.4% higher, i.e., favourable to the reference).

Effect sizes are interpreted against a **historical threshold of 11.3%**. The value was fixed early in the study as a working scale and is reused unchanged; its original derivation is not documented, so we describe it as a heuristic of this study rather than as a measured property. A post hoc reconstruction from archived per-seed values is retained in the research archive: for each of 20 (unit × context-sampling-mode) groups on the history feature set, three seeds were run with nothing else changed, and the mean relative range

of per-cell RMSE across those groups is

. The agreement is consistent with the threshold’s origin but does not establish it. Single-run differences below the threshold are not treated as findings. The threshold is a per-seed quantity: paired designs and twenty-seed means resolve smaller differences, and win counts are sign counts, so a 1–2% entry can decide a unit’s sign without itself being claimed as an effect.

For the main comparison we test our per-seed values against the ten published per-experiment values with two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests, apply Holm correction across the 11 units within each backbone, and report bootstrap 95% confidence intervals of the relative difference. The corrected family is therefore composed of units, not of held-out cells, and a unit’s weight in it is not proportional to its statistical power: the underlying test is against the ten published per-experiment values regardless of how many cells the unit holds out, and held-out counts range from two to twenty-three ([Appendix A](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#app1-energies-19-04805)). Five seeds per unit permit, but do not guarantee, a significant result against the ten published values: with

Observed effects include small changes below the 11.3% descriptive noise floor (including significant GBDT improvements of 5.5% and 6.2%); that floor is not a significance threshold. Where an effect is of the order of the floor, as in [Section 3.9](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot9-energies-19-04805) and [Section 3.11](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot11-energies-19-04805), we use twenty seeds instead.

### 2.6. Randomization-Based Validation Design

Feature-level claims are easily confounded, so the mechanism is tested with a three-arm randomized design in the tradition of randomization inference [24,25].

*   **Treatment arm**: the true origin-self offset,

. 
*   **Mispairing arm**: the anchor is replaced by the first cycle of a randomly assigned other cell, using a fixed-seed derangement (no fixed points). The multiset of anchors is therefore unchanged, as are the arithmetic and the dimensionality of the transform: the same first cycles are reassigned. What changes is which origin each cycle is compared against, and with it the joint distribution of the resulting difference. This arm rules out the alternative that any difference feature would serve, whatever it is referenced to.

*   **Instrument arm**: the anchor is the mean first cycle of the source cells, a per-column constant whose effect should be near zero for translation-invariant tree splits with matched randomness; finite implementations need not be exactly invariant. This is a negative control on the measurement apparatus itself [26]: its measured value tests the harness rather than the method, since a non-zero reading would mean the pipeline manufactures differences of its own.

For permutation inference we repeat the mispairing arm under M independent derangements and compute the Monte Carlo randomization p-value

, where T is the unit-averaged RMSE. We use

for GBDT across all 11 units, and

for TabPFN on three representative units (chosen to span both carrier regimes of [Section 3.10](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot10-energies-19-04805)), reusing the identical derangements so that the two families are directly comparable. These are sampled randomization probabilities with the plus-one correction, not exhaustive enumeration of all possible anchor assignments; the minimum attainable values are 1/101 and 1/21.

Feature attribution uses permutation importance on held-out cells, aggregated by family, to describe fitted-model reliance on the feature families.

What the design does and does not establish. The manipulation acts on the feature matrix, not on the ageing process: the arithmetic and the dimensionality of the difference feature are held fixed and the anchor values are held fixed as a multiset, so what is broken is only the pairing between a cycle and the origin it is referenced to. The result is a randomization test of a representation claim, in the sense of permutation inference [24,25], and it supports statements of the form “the self-pairing, and not generic differencing, carries the gain”. It does not identify a mechanism of electrochemical degradation, it does not estimate an interventional effect on a cell, and Pearl-style identification and do-calculus are outside its scope; the instrument arm is a negative control on the measurement harness rather than on the physical system [26]. Statements elsewhere in this paper about physical behaviour are mechanistic interpretations of that randomization result, not independent causal claims.

### 2.7. Pre-Registration and Reproducibility

Following pre-registration practice [27], 24 of the original 44 experiments carry 34 decision criteria in their frozen docstrings; this is not a claim that every experiment was pre-registered. Their documents are retained in the archive. The experiments added during this revision also state their criteria in the script docstring, and we grade their evidence rather than pooling it. For the scripts written first (the strict-reader re-run, the cost measurement, the curve-feature extraction and the two simple-baseline experiments), the commit carrying the criteria precedes the commit carrying the results, so the criteria are verifiable in the version history. One script family is weaker evidence: the TabPFN re-run on the second device shares its commit with its result, because it re-executes an already-frozen protocol on different hardware rather than introducing a new criterion. We therefore claim prior registration only for the former group. Other revision analyses whose script and first result share a commit (including bexp50–55) have explicit criteria but no separately timestamped pre-run commit; we do not claim prior registration for them. All outcomes, including falsified hypotheses, are retained in the research archive. Implementation corrections made during this revision carry per-script version notes rather than new criteria: the row-mapping rewrite and the CC/CV window correction of the raw-data manifest and curve extraction, the corrected MIT unit convention, the identity-gate exclusion in the baseline experiments, and the warm-up fix in the cost measurement; superseded archives are retained next to their replacements. Two of the notes record post-diagnostic rule corrections rather than pure implementation fixes—the cycle-duration quality gate and the identity-coverage threshold were added after inspecting the mapping diagnostics—and are marked as such in the scripts. Thirteen criteria relevant to this manuscript—those frozen in the previous revision, i.e., before the implementation corrections above—were not met, and the corresponding claims were downgraded or retracted rather than quietly re-worded. From the original study: the convention–importance correlation ([Section 3.1](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot1-energies-19-04805)), the five-seed win count ([Section 3.9](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot9-energies-19-04805)), the learned-anchor improvement and its autonomy variant ([Section 3.11](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot11-energies-19-04805)), the two distillation criteria and the injection criterion ([Section 4.5](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec4dot5-energies-19-04805)). From this revision: two criteria that required the archived importance table to be reproduced within ten percentage points, by the ten-seed tree arm and by the foundation-model arm ([Section 3.10](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot10-energies-19-04805)); two that required the corrected deployable pipeline to show an accuracy gain for the compact set and for the full stack under gradient boosting ([Section 3.7](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot7-energies-19-04805)); the corresponding criterion under the frozen foundation model ([Section 3.7](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot7-energies-19-04805)); and the fleet-cost criterion under its primary amortised reading ([Section 3.7](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot7-energies-19-04805)).

## 3. Results

Multiplying the measured model-prediction medians by 10,000 rows gives 28–43 s at a batch of one, or 0.037–0.051 s in batches of 1024; these arithmetic scenarios exclude fitting, feature acquisition and construction, scheduling and data transfer, and are not measured fleet runtimes. We report these as compute times only: no energy or carbon figure is derived from them, and no per-cell figure for the archived foundation-model runs ([Section 2.4](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec2dot4-energies-19-04805)). The companion benchmark’s deep baselines take about 170 s per run on their own hardware. Embedded feasibility is therefore left unverified: no measurement on microcontroller-class hardware was performed, and a pretrained transformer of this size is not in that class, so we claim neither embedded nor fleet-scale throughput. [Section 3.8](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot8-energies-19-04805) reports the two validated simple references that a deployment-minded reader would try first.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1. Carrier Attribution: Scope and Counterexamples

The carrier pattern of [Table 14](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#energies-19-04805-t014) is consistent with HUST and TJU-3, where life-fraction importance is high and the explicit offset provides little benefit in the legacy controls. It does not predict the sign of the benefit across units. XJTU-RW has 93.8% life-fraction importance, yet replacing the cycle column reduces error by 14.1% ([Table 2](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#energies-19-04805-t002)), and true self-pairing reduces error by 8.9% against a 15.4% increase when mispaired ([Table 12](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#energies-19-04805-t012)). XJTU-3C and XJTU-R2.5 also improve despite high life-fraction shares (75.7% and 79.2%). Permutation importance describes reliance of a fitted model under one protocol; it neither establishes redundant information nor supplies a general rule for when SRL helps. We therefore restrict the carrier explanation to the observations it fits and report these counterexamples explicitly.

Deployment removes the life-fraction channel because total life is unknown while a cell is in service, whereas the own-cell offset remains computable from retained records. Whether that improves accuracy is a separate question. The corrected evaluation of [Section 3.7](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot7-energies-19-04805) answers it negatively for the learners tested: removing target-future and label information from the reader does not meet the cross-unit improvement criterion for any explicit-reference arm. The offline attribution therefore does not establish an accuracy advantage after deployment.

The anchor experiments further delimit the legacy result: discarding the first ten retained cycles removes the median benefit under the tested offline protocol ([Table 15](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#energies-19-04805-t015)). This supports retaining the earliest recorded cycle for that comparison; it does not identify a manufacturing state or establish a strict-online accuracy advantage.

Subtracting a cell’s earliest record can remove a time-invariant component of its charge-segment statistics and retain changes associated with ageing. This is a physical interpretation of the representation, not an identity with the target. In the released primary benchmark and our adapter, SOH is cycle discharge capacity divided by the dataset’s nominal capacity,

, not by the measured capacity of that cell’s earliest record. It therefore need not equal one at the anchor. A reference from another cell can introduce between-cell offsets; this interpretation is consistent with the legacy mispairing result but does not identify an electrochemical degradation mechanism or imply strict-online accuracy improvement.

BatLiNet [31] distinguishes intra-cell early-cycle differences from inter-cell references to predict battery lifetime across ageing conditions. Our task is cycle-level SOH estimation, and our legacy control reassigns own-cell anchors while holding their values fixed. Its result concerns that representation under its reader and learner, not the usefulness of inter-cell learning on a different prediction task; it does not contradict BatLiNet’s learned inter-cell reference.

### 4.2. Design Implications of the Negative Results

The negative results constrain three separate interpretations. First, the offline anchor probes favour the earliest retained record over the tested delayed and learned anchors; they do not show that later cycles cannot reconstruct a useful reference in general. Second, the tested teacher-target blending scheme does not meet its improvement criterion: the student improves on only 3 of 11 units and the median error increases with blending weight ([Figure 4](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#energies-19-04805-f004)c). The mixed directions of the legacy cycle-convention contrast do not identify why blending fails because they concern a different manipulation and row protocol. Third, the corrected reader shows that compact SRL is not uniformly harmless: for TabPFN it raises error on XJTU-3C ([Table 10](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#energies-19-04805-t010)). A deployment evaluation must therefore specify retained inputs, anchor availability and model-specific accuracy rather than assume the offline advantage transfers.

### 4.3. Comparability with Published Numbers

The XJTU dataset has an officially maintained summary of published results [32] spanning dozens of 2025–2026 papers, with reported RMSE values as low as 0.23%. That summary also records that evaluation modes differ across papers (intra-cell interpolation versus cross-cell estimation, single batch versus all batches, and non-uniform splits), and its maintainers do not rank across papers. The XJTU-family studies cited in the Introduction were not audited paper by paper in this revision: we did not verify, from their full texts or released code, that they adopt the official splits or the per-cell normalization of the release used here, so we place no numbers side by side with theirs, and the comparability statement below is restricted to results published under the reference protocol itself. We follow the same discipline: comparisons are made only within protocols aligned position by position, by reading the reference implementation’s scoring code and by executing its training code. Our per-unit RMSE values (0.43–1.32%) are of the same magnitude as recent reports while being obtained entirely in the cross-cell mode under fixed official splits, which is the harder setting. One preprint [8] reports values below ours on the same four public libraries (387 cells), but it reports batch-level point values without run-to-run distributions, discloses neither its cell-level splits nor its code, and its chemistry description (four chemistries including LCO) is inconsistent with the official releases of these libraries (NCM, NCA and LFP; [Appendix A](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#app1-energies-19-04805)); it is therefore cited without comparison. The follow-up studies cited in [Section 1](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec1-energies-19-04805) are not uniformly comparable at this granularity: they do not all state whether the released cell-level splits are retained, and some report pooled rather than per-experiment error. We therefore restrict quantitative comparison to the protocol whose splits and scoring code we could verify directly [1].

### 4.4. Implications for Battery Management in Sustainable Power Systems

Three consequences concern potential deployment. First, source-trained default gradient boosting and frozen-model inference provide baselines that do not require target-cell fitting; under the corrected reader, adding SRL does not establish an overall accuracy advantage. Second, CPU cost was measured for separate workloads: fitting the gradient-boosted model takes under one second, its single-row prediction 2.8–4.3 ms, and the frozen foundation model takes 1.3–60 s per unit × view × seed including context preparation ([Section 3.7](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#sec3dot7-energies-19-04805)). The sub-second fitting measurement belongs only to GBDT. Third, reproducing this SRL requires retaining the earliest valid charge-segment record with cell identity; doing so preserves feature availability, not a demonstrated online accuracy benefit. No microcontroller measurement or field deployment was performed. Prior deployment-oriented work has examined data sufficiency and short observation windows [33]; our reader audit concerns a separate constraint, the information available at scoring time.

### 4.5. Limitations

The claims of this paper hold under five stated conditions, which distinguish protocol-specific results from deployment hypotheses.

**Anchor availability.** The implemented SRL requires an earliest retained own-cell record. [Table 15](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#energies-19-04805-t015) shows that discarding ten retained records removes the median benefit under the legacy offline reader. This does not prove that SRL is unusable for all second-life cells or fleets without commissioning data: a later observed anchor remains computable, but reproduces a different representation whose accuracy is unvalidated here. The corrected-online result must be distinguished from this offline sensitivity probe.

**Model-family coverage.** A tuned MLP improves on only 5 of 11 units; adding offsets to the companion benchmark’s own MLP improves on only 1 of 6 batches. These observations do not identify why those networks fail to exploit the offsets. The positive full-stack comparisons for GBDT and TabPFN are offline and include learner effects; neither supports a universal model-family or deployment accuracy claim.

**The tested transfer scheme did not meet its criterion.** Motivated by the offline carrier pattern, an obvious remedy is to train on that channel and transfer what it knows to a deployable model. This is legitimate: complete trajectories of the training cells are available, so the constraint binds only at inference on the target cell (learning under privileged information [34]), and we tested it: a teacher using the life-fraction carrier supplied out-of-fold soft targets that were blended into an online-computable student’s regression objective. The tested blending scheme did not meet its improvement criterion. The student improved on only 3 of 11 units, the median change was

at a blending weight of 0.5, and degradation grew monotonically with that weight (

,

,

at 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7, [Figure 4](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#energies-19-04805-f004)c). Per-unit Mann–Whitney tests with Holm correction leave one unit significantly better and none significantly worse, so the accurate reading is that this scheme did not meet its improvement criterion; neither universal transfer failure nor significant harm is established. One pattern is consistent with [Table 2](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/19/20/4805#energies-19-04805-t002): the offline cycle convention is not a universal upper bound: it is better than the fixed-scale convention on 5 of 11 units, but exceeds the 11.3% noise floor on only 4 (XJTU-2C, XJTU-R2.5, TJU-3 and HUST), and is worse on the other 6. These are legacy convention diagnostics, not corrected-online comparisons, and do not identify the reason target blending fails. Whether a different transfer mechanism succeeds where target blending fails is open, and identifying the failure mechanism would require a different probe.

**Statistical power at the unit level.** Cell-level generalization is limited by the two or three held-out cells in each XJTU batch and TJU-3. The legacy per-unit pairing effect-to-noise ratios span 0.5–11.9, and six of eleven units reach

; the pooled randomization result concerns an average across units. These pairing tests differ from the run-level comparison against the published baseline: in the latter, two units per backbone are non-significant, with three distinct units in their union (TJU-2 for both, XJTU-RW for TabPFN, TJU-1 for GBDT). Non-significance does not establish equivalence.

**Scope of the data and of the claim.** All libraries are laboratory ageing data acquired under controlled conditions; field data with dynamic loads, temperature excursions, and calendar ageing is not covered. All claims are scoped to battery SOH estimation, where the benchmark target is cycle capacity divided by nominal capacity; we make no claim about degradation tasks whose labels lack that structure.

## 5. Conclusions

What a feature is compared against (other cells, a recent past, or the earliest retained record) is a design decision that has been made implicitly inside normalization choices, and it materially affects error under the tested legacy protocol: replacing a single column’s convention moves error from

to

across eleven evaluation units. The benchmark SOH label uses nominal capacity as its denominator, while the prevailing per-cell convention depends on full-lifetime extrema that a cell in service cannot know. We made the comparison explicit and online-computable as a self-referenced lift.

Under the offline author protocol, off-the-shelf learners with the full history stack are significantly more accurate than the physics-informed network on 9 of 11 units for each of two backbones, with none significantly worse. Under the corrected strict-online reader, compact SRL instead changes median per-cell RMSE by

for gradient boosting (5 of 11 improve) and

for the frozen foundation model (3 of 11 improve); feature computability does not translate into an overall accuracy gain. The companion benchmark improves on five published deep baselines in 6 of 6 batches with per-batch selection (5 of 6 with a fixed configuration), but its normalization already supplies an implicit comparison. The unseen-library 22–33% improvement and the randomized pairing results (

for gradient boosting,

for the frozen foundation model) are legacy diagnostics under their stated readers. Tree-model carrier attribution is consistent with HUST and TJU-3 but has counterexamples and does not establish a universal mechanism.

For sustainable power and energy systems the practical message is economical rather than architectural. The measured CPU workloads have seconds-per-unit cost without training on the target system; the SRL feature contract additionally requires each cell’s earliest record to be retained, with the cost profiles of the two backbones reported as separate measurements of different workloads and neither presented as an embedded-deployment claim. Three qualifications travel with that statement. The deployability result is negative in our corrected evaluation: with target-future and label information removed from the reader, the reference families do not meet the stated cross-unit improvement criterion, so what survives is the feature contract rather than an accuracy claim. Every accuracy figure is obtained on laboratory ageing data under controlled protocols, so what is established is that each required quantity is computable at time t in service, not that the same accuracy transfers to field BMS conditions with dynamic loads, calendar ageing and sensor drift; extending the analysis to such field data, and to fleet-level deployment where per-cell origin records and cross-fleet heterogeneity interact, is left to future work. The whole account also presumes a retained commissioning record: without it, the reference cannot be reconstructed after the fact. We recommend that the comparison target be reported as a standard ablation in degradation modelling, that evaluation protocols distinguish channels available offline from those that survive deployment, and that commissioning records be treated as a functional part of the health-estimation pipeline.
