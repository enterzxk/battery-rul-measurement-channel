Abstract
Battery health estimation depends on feature references as well as learners. Changing
one column’s normalization moves root-mean-square error (RMSE) by up to 108%. We
construct a self-referenced lift (SRL) from each cell’s earliest retained record. Under the
offline author protocol, learners with the full history stack improve on 9 of 11 units after
Holm correction, with none significantly worse and two units per backbone not significantly
different. Relative-change medians over all 11 units are −16.8% for the frozen foundation
model and −19.1% for gradient boosting, including learner effects. Under a strict online
reader excluding target-future and label information, compact SRL instead yields +4.3% for
gradient boosting (5 of 11 improve) and +10.4% for the frozen foundation model (3 of
11 improve): no overall online accuracy gain. Legacy offline pairing controls favour
self-pairing (p = 0.0099, 100 derangements). A companion benchmark with implicit
self-comparison improves in 6 of 6 batches with per-batch selection (5 of 6 with a fixed
configuration); an unseen-library legacy diagnostic improves by 22–33%. Deployability
concerns feature computability, not a strict-online accuracy advantage.
Keywords: battery state of health; lithium-ion batteries; feature engineering; self-referenced
representation; tabular foundation model; battery management system
1. Introduction
Battery energy storage is central to sustainable power systems: it absorbs renewable
variability, provides frequency and capacity services, and extends the life of costly, carbon-
intensive assets. All these functions depend on knowing how healthy each cell is. State-of-
health (SOH) estimation is thus not merely a diagnostic convenience but an operational
and economic requirement for battery management systems (BMS) in grid-scale storage
and electrified transport: accurate estimates enable deeper usable state-of-charge windows,
better-timed maintenance, and defensible second-life decisions, reducing lifetime cost and
material footprint.
Data-driven SOH estimation has advanced rapidly: a prominent line embeds empiri-
cal degradation equations into neural networks as soft constraints, establishing the four
processed libraries, official cell-level splits, and per-experiment results used here [1], while a
companion benchmark supplies five deep baselines under three input types [2]. It has since
grown through 2025–2026 with physics-informed variants [3–5], physics-embedded trans-
fer learning [6], few-shot cross-domain frameworks [7], and quantum-feature hybrids [8]. A

Nearly all of this work rests on a rarely examined assumption: the representation
fed to the model—per-cycle statistical features under standard normalization—is settled
preprocessing. Two measurements contradict it: first, with data, model, split and every
other column fixed and only the cycle-index normalization convention changed, pooled-
cycle RMSE moves by +107.8% on one evaluation unit, −49.6% on another, and −0.5% on
a third; second, the benefit of an explicit self-referenced feature on the 67-dimensional
feature set reverses sign, from −5.3% to +4.5%, depending solely on whether features
are normalized per cell or against source-fitted statistics. Normalization is where the
representation’s comparison target is decided, and it materially affects accuracy under the
tested protocol.
The motivation is a feature-reference question, not an identity imposed by the label:
the benchmark SOH is present discharge capacity divided by nominal capacity, not the
measured first-cycle capacity. Comparing each unit against its own baseline is standard
practice in other longitudinal settings: change-from-baseline and ANCOVA analyses in
clinical trials [21], within (fixed-effect) transformations in panel econometrics [22], and
explicit handling of unknown initial wear in prognostics datasets [23]. Battery pipelines
are implicitly consistent with this: normalizing each cell by its own full-lifetime extrema
compares every cell against itself. That convention suits offline assessment with complete
trajectories, but cannot be reproduced in service, because cells differ widely in lifetime and
a cell in operation does not know its own lifetime extrema. Total life is not the cycle-level
SOH target studied here; nevertheless, the offline-helpful comparison is unavailable online.
This paper makes the comparison explicit, minimal, and deployment-computable, then
tests—with a randomized mispairing control rather than a causal identification argument—
whether the pairing itself is responsible for the observed gains. Our contributions are
as follows.
1.
We identify and quantify what features are compared against as a first-order design
variable in degradation feature engineering, hidden inside routine normalization
choices, and show its effect spans −49.6% to +107.8% across eleven evaluation units.
2.
We define the self-referenced lift (SRL), an explicit, deployment-computable, model-
agnostic feature family, and separate its computability from its accuracy. The offline
author protocol yields Holm-significant improvements over a physics-informed net-
work on 9 of 11 units for each of two backbones, including learner effects; the corrected
strict online evaluation does not reproduce an overall accuracy gain from adding com-
pact SRL.

2. Materials and Methods
For readers coming from an electrochemistry background, the machine-learning vo-
cabulary used below is fixed as follows. Cross-cell estimation means that the model is fitted
on cells whose full trajectories are available and tested on cells it has never seen; the latter
are the held-out cells of an official split, reserved by the dataset authors. Primary-benchmark
settings are not selected on them; the companion best-of-four summary is explicitly a post
hoc choice using held-out scores and is separated from its fixed configuration (Section 3.5).
An evaluation unit is the group of cells over which results are averaged, here one ageing
batch or one library. A feature family is a set of columns obtained from one and the same
comparison; an ablation removes one family at a time to measure what it contributes. Permu-
tation importance measures how much error rises when a column’s values are shuffled, and
a permutation test asks whether an observed difference survives relabelling. A derangement
is a relabelling with no element left in place, and a negative control is an arm whose true
effect is known to be zero, so that it can only reveal artefacts of the measurement itself.
2.1. Datasets and Splits
The primary benchmark consists of the four processed libraries released with the
reference physics-informed work [1]: XJTU (six ageing batches), TJU (three batches) [28],
HUST [29], and MIT [9], giving 11 evaluation units in total (387 cells, 82 of them held out);
the MIT unit pools the three original sub-batches of that release (12 May 2017, 30 June 2017
and 12 April 2018) into a single unit, so it is not broken out per sub-batch as the XJTU
batches are. The libraries span three cathode chemistries (NCM, NCA and LFP), with one
batch that mixes two of them, and nominal capacities from 1.1 to 3.5 Ah; Appendix A
lists chemistry, nominal capacity, cell counts, per-cell cycle-count ranges and the lowest
observed SOH for every unit. We adopt the official cell-level splits without modification;
held-out cells are never used for fitting or model selection; normalization statistics follow
the pipeline and are stated per pipeline below. In particular, Pipeline A scales each cell by
its own full-lifetime extrema, which is same-cell future information that a cell in service
cannot know; we treat that convention as offline-only and never pool its numbers with
Pipeline B. The secondary benchmark is the companion deep-learning benchmark [2] on
XJTU (55 cells, 67 handcrafted features, leave-one-cell-out splits). For external verification,
we use an independent library, MATR-CLO (45 cells), the closed-loop-optimisation batch
of Attia et al. [30]. It is disjoint from the three MIT batches in the primary benchmark
and from the MATR batch used during development, and it took part in no design or

2.3. Two Evaluation Pipelines
Because the central issue is what can be computed in service, all experiments are run
in one of two clearly separated pipelines, and numbers from the two are never pooled.
Pipeline A (reference convention, offline). Normalization follows the reference protocol:
each cell is min–max scaled by its own full-lifetime extrema, and the cycle column is min–
max scaled by the cell’s full retained length. Its author-offline reader also filters rows using
full-lifetime statistics including the capacity label (Section 3.7). This pipeline is used for
like-for-like comparison against published results, since both sides then operate under
identical rules.
Pipeline B (deployment-computable). Normalization statistics are fitted on source cells
only and applied unchanged to held-out cells; the cycle column is scaled by a fixed constant
((t −1)/200) so that it does not depend on total life; all other features remain history-only.
Each Pipeline B input is computable once the current cycle’s required charge segment has
completed; this is cycle-level availability, not continuous estimation during a partial cycle.

Effect sizes are interpreted against a historical threshold of 11.3%. The value was
fixed early in the study as a working scale and is reused unchanged; its original derivation
is not documented, so we describe it as a heuristic of this study rather than as a measured
property. A post hoc reconstruction from archived per-seed values is retained in the
research archive: for each of 20 (unit × context-sampling-mode) groups on the history
feature set, three seeds were run with nothing else changed, and the mean relative range
(max −min)/mean of per-cell RMSE across those groups is 11.35%. The agreement is
consistent with the threshold’s origin but does not establish it. Single-run differences below
the threshold are not treated as findings. The threshold is a per-seed quantity: paired
designs and twenty-seed means resolve smaller differences, and win counts are sign counts,
so a 1–2% entry can decide a unit’s sign without itself being claimed as an effect.
For the main comparison we test our per-seed values against the ten published per-
experiment values with two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests, apply Holm correction across
the 11 units within each backbone, and report bootstrap 95% confidence intervals of the
relative difference. The corrected family is therefore composed of units, not of held-out
cells, and a unit’s weight in it is not proportional to its statistical power: the underlying
test is against the ten published per-experiment values regardless of how many cells the
unit holds out, and held-out counts range from two to twenty-three (Appendix A). Five
seeds per unit permit, but do not guarantee, a significant result against the ten published
values: with 5 × 10 observations the smallest attainable two-sided p is 6.7 × 10−4, which
can survive Holm correction across eleven units. Observed effects include small changes
below the 11.3% descriptive noise floor (including significant GBDT improvements of 5.5%
and 6.2%); that floor is not a significance threshold. Where an effect is of the order of the
floor, as in Sections 3.9 and 3.11, we use twenty seeds instead.
2.6. Randomization-Based Validation Design
Feature-level claims are easily confounded, so the mechanism is tested with a three-
arm randomized design in the tradition of randomization inference [24,25].
•
Treatment arm: the true origin-self offset, xt −x1.
•
Mispairing arm: the anchor is replaced by the first cycle of a randomly assigned other
cell, using a fixed-seed derangement (no fixed points). The multiset of anchors is
therefore unchanged, as are the arithmetic and the dimensionality of the transform: the
same first cycles are reassigned. What changes is which origin each cycle is compared
against, and with it the joint distribution of the resulting difference. This arm rules out
the alternative that any difference feature would serve, whatever it is referenced to.
•
Instrument arm: the anchor is the mean first cycle of the source cells, a per-column
constant whose effect should be near zero for translation-invariant tree splits with
matched randomness; finite implementations need not be exactly invariant. This is a
negative control on the measurement apparatus itself [26]: its measured value tests
the harness rather than the method, since a non-zero reading would mean the pipeline
manufactures differences of its own.
For permutation inference we repeat the mispairing arm under M independent
derangements and compute the Monte Carlo randomization p-value p = (1 + #{m :
Tm ≤Tself})/(M + 1), where T is the unit-averaged RMSE. We use M = 100 for GBDT

What the design does and does not establish. The manipulation acts on the feature
matrix, not on the ageing process: the arithmetic and the dimensionality of the difference
feature are held fixed and the anchor values are held fixed as a multiset, so what is broken
is only the pairing between a cycle and the origin it is referenced to. The result is a
randomization test of a representation claim, in the sense of permutation inference [24,25],
and it supports statements of the form “the self-pairing, and not generic differencing,
carries the gain”. It does not identify a mechanism of electrochemical degradation, it does
not estimate an interventional effect on a cell, and Pearl-style identification and do-calculus
are outside its scope; the instrument arm is a negative control on the measurement harness
rather than on the physical system [26]. Statements elsewhere in this paper about physical
behaviour are mechanistic interpretations of that randomization result, not independent
causal claims.
2.7. Pre-Registration and Reproducibility
Following pre-registration practice [27], 24 of the original 44 experiments carry 34 de-
cision criteria in their frozen docstrings; this is not a claim that every experiment was
pre-registered. Their documents are retained in the archive. The experiments added during
this revision also state their criteria in the script docstring, and we grade their evidence
rather than pooling it. For the scripts written first (the strict-reader re-run, the cost measure-

carrying the criteria precedes the commit carrying the results, so the criteria are verifiable

protocol on different hardware rather than introducing a new criterion. We therefore claim
prior registration only for the former group. Other revision analyses whose script and
first result share a commit (including bexp50–55) have explicit criteria but no separately
timestamped pre-run commit; we do not claim prior registration for them. All outcomes,
including falsified hypotheses, are retained in the research archive. Implementation cor-

the row-mapping rewrite and the CC/CV window correction of the raw-data manifest

3. Results

3.4. How Much of the Margin Is the Representation?
The comparison of Section 3.3 changes several things at once, so we separate them
here: Table 6 presents two further rungs of the baseline ladder that distinguish the SRL
columns’ contribution from the learner’s, and two deliberately simple references place the
machine-learning numbers in a familiar frame.
Under the author protocol the explicit offset does not help a linear model (median
+4.1%, better on 4 of 11 units) but does help the tree model (−5.6%, 9 of 11); under the
corrected deployable reader it helps neither (+0.8% and 5 of 11 for ridge, +4.3% and 5
of 11 for gradient boosting, from the same archive that Section 3.7 reports in full). The
earlier positive entries came from the superseded preprocessing of Section 3.7 and are no

SRL features, gradient boosting beats ridge by about 36% under the author protocol and
25% under the corrected reader (relative to the ridge value), so the learner choice matters
a great deal. We do not, however, turn that gap into a share of the margin against the
physics-informed network, because the two comparisons differ in protocol, training budget

additively using a gap measured under another. What the tables support is the weaker

3.7. Deployment-Computable Pipeline and Computational Cost
Table 10 reports Pipeline B re-run with a corrected reader that cannot see the target’s
future. The earlier implementation of both pipelines filtered rows using each cell’s full-
lifetime statistics including its capacity label, and its anchor was the first row surviving that
filter rather than the first recorded cycle; measured on the released files, those anchors fall
on records 1 to 4, and the filter changes the row set as well. Because that procedure uses
target-future and label information, it cannot support a deployment claim. The corrected
reader keeps a row only when the current row’s inputs are finite, so nothing about the
target’s future enters either the row set or the anchor; the source-fitted normalization and
the fixed-scale cycle column are unchanged.
In this corrected configuration the explicit reference families do not reach the pre-
registered bar for a uniform gain, and their direction depends on the model and the feature
set. Relative to raw features of the same backbone, the compact SRL set changes the median
per-cell RMSE by +4.3% and improves 5 of 11 units for gradient boosting, and by +10.4%
and 3 of 11 for the frozen foundation model; the full 81-dimensional stack gives +0.3% and
5 of 11 for gradient boosting and −1.2% and 6 of 11 for the foundation model; ridge shows
the same pattern as the tree model (+0.8%, 5 of 11). Against a pre-registered criterion
requiring improvement on at least 7 of 11 units, every arm fails, and we report the result
as it stands. Direction is not uniform across models either: the foundation model pays
substantially for the compact set while gaining slightly from the full stack. On XJTU-3C
the compact set inflates that model’s error by +154% (0.00780 to 0.01980) while the same
columns move the tree model by +8%—the largest regression in Table 10—so the in-context
learner is markedly more sensitive to the added offset block than the tree ensemble is. The
earlier numbers are retained as a historical record and are not used. The corrected result

4. Discussion
4.1. Carrier Attribution: Scope and Counterexamples
The carrier pattern of Table 14 is consistent with HUST and TJU-3, where life-fraction
importance is high and the explicit offset provides little benefit in the legacy controls. It does
not predict the sign of the benefit across units. XJTU-RW has 93.8% life-fraction importance,
yet replacing the cycle column reduces error by 14.1% (Table 2), and true self-pairing reduces
error by 8.9% against a 15.4% increase when mispaired (Table 12). XJTU-3C and XJTU-R2.5
also improve despite high life-fraction shares (75.7% and 79.2%). Permutation importance
describes reliance of a fitted model under one protocol; it neither establishes redundant
information nor supplies a general rule for when SRL helps. We therefore restrict the carrier
explanation to the observations it fits and report these counterexamples explicitly.
Deployment removes the life-fraction channel because total life is unknown while a
cell is in service, whereas the own-cell offset remains computable from retained records.
Whether that improves accuracy is a separate question. The corrected evaluation of
Section 3.7 answers it negatively for the learners tested: removing target-future and label
information from the reader does not meet the cross-unit improvement criterion for any
explicit-reference arm. The offline attribution therefore does not establish an accuracy
advantage after deployment.
The anchor experiments further delimit the legacy result: discarding the first ten
retained cycles removes the median benefit under the tested offline protocol (Table 15).
This supports retaining the earliest recorded cycle for that comparison; it does not identify
a manufacturing state or establish a strict-online accuracy advantage.
Subtracting a cell’s earliest record can remove a time-invariant component of its
charge-segment statistics and retain changes associated with ageing. This is a physical
interpretation of the representation, not an identity with the target. In the released primary
benchmark and our adapter, SOH is cycle discharge capacity divided by the dataset’s
nominal capacity, Qt/Qnom, not by the measured capacity of that cell’s earliest record. It
therefore need not equal one at the anchor. A reference from another cell can introduce
between-cell offsets; this interpretation is consistent with the legacy mispairing result
but does not identify an electrochemical degradation mechanism or imply strict-online
accuracy improvement.
BatLiNet [31] distinguishes intra-cell early-cycle differences from inter-cell references
to predict battery lifetime across ageing conditions. Our task is cycle-level SOH estimation,
and our legacy control reassigns own-cell anchors while holding their values fixed. Its
result concerns that representation under its reader and learner, not the usefulness of
https://doi.org/10.3390/en19204805

===== PAGE 23 =====
Energies 2026, 19, 4805
23 of 29
inter-cell learning on a different prediction task; it does not contradict BatLiNet’s learned
inter-cell reference.
4.2. Design Implications of the Negative Results
The negative results constrain three separate interpretations. First, the offline anchor
probes favour the earliest retained record over the tested delayed and learned anchors;
they do not show that later cycles cannot reconstruct a useful reference in general. Second,
the tested teacher-target blending scheme does not meet its improvement criterion: the
student improves on only 3 of 11 units and the median error increases with blending
weight (Figure 4c). The mixed directions of the legacy cycle-convention contrast do not
identify why blending fails because they concern a different manipulation and row protocol.
Third, the corrected reader shows that compact SRL is not uniformly harmless: for TabPFN
it raises error on XJTU-3C (Table 10). A deployment evaluation must therefore specify
retained inputs, anchor availability and model-specific accuracy rather than assume the
offline advantage transfers.
4.3. Comparability with Published Numbers
The XJTU dataset has an officially maintained summary of published results [32]
spanning dozens of 2025–2026 papers, with reported RMSE values as low as 0.23%. That
summary also records that evaluation modes differ across papers (intra-cell interpolation
versus cross-cell estimation, single batch versus all batches, and non-uniform splits), and its
maintainers do not rank across papers. The XJTU-family studies cited in the Introduction
were not audited paper by paper in this revision: we did not verify, from their full texts
or released code, that they adopt the official splits or the per-cell normalization of the
release used here, so we place no numbers side by side with theirs, and the comparability
statement below is restricted to results published under the reference protocol itself. We
follow the same discipline: comparisons are made only within protocols aligned position
by position, by reading the reference implementation’s scoring code and by executing
its training code. Our per-unit RMSE values (0.43–1.32%) are of the same magnitude as
recent reports while being obtained entirely in the cross-cell mode under fixed official
splits, which is the harder setting. One preprint [8] reports values below ours on the same
four public libraries (387 cells), but it reports batch-level point values without run-to-run
distributions, discloses neither its cell-level splits nor its code, and its chemistry description
(four chemistries including LCO) is inconsistent with the official releases of these libraries
(NCM, NCA and LFP; Appendix A); it is therefore cited without comparison. The follow-up
studies cited in Section 1 are not uniformly comparable at this granularity: they do not all
state whether the released cell-level splits are retained, and some report pooled rather than
per-experiment error. We therefore restrict quantitative comparison to the protocol whose
splits and scoring code we could verify directly [1].
4.4. Implications for Battery Management in Sustainable Power Systems
Three consequences concern potential deployment. First, source-trained default gradi-
ent boosting and frozen-model inference provide baselines that do not require target-cell
fitting; under the corrected reader, adding SRL does not establish an overall accuracy
advantage. Second, CPU cost was measured for separate workloads: fitting the gradient-
boosted model takes under one second, its single-row prediction 2.8–4.3 ms, and the frozen
foundation model takes 1.3–60 s per unit × view × seed including context preparation
(Section 3.7). The sub-second fitting measurement belongs only to GBDT. Third, reproduc-
ing this SRL requires retaining the earliest valid charge-segment record with cell identity;
doing so preserves feature availability, not a demonstrated online accuracy benefit. No mi-
crocontroller measurement or field deployment was performed. Prior deployment-oriented
https://doi.org/10.3390/en19204805

Energies 2026, 19, 4805
24 of 29
work has examined data sufficiency and short observation windows [33]; our reader audit
concerns a separate constraint, the information available at scoring time.
4.5. Limitations
The claims of this paper hold under five stated conditions, which distinguish protocol-
specific results from deployment hypotheses.
Anchor availability. The implemented SRL requires an earliest retained own-cell
record. Table 15 shows that discarding ten retained records removes the median benefit
under the legacy offline reader. This does not prove that SRL is unusable for all second-life
cells or fleets without commissioning data: a later observed anchor remains computable, but
reproduces a different representation whose accuracy is unvalidated here. The corrected-
online result must be distinguished from this offline sensitivity probe.
Model-family coverage. A tuned MLP improves on only 5 of 11 units; adding offsets to
the companion benchmark’s own MLP improves on only 1 of 6 batches. These observations
do not identify why those networks fail to exploit the offsets. The positive full-stack
comparisons for GBDT and TabPFN are offline and include learner effects; neither supports
a universal model-family or deployment accuracy claim.
The tested transfer scheme did not meet its criterion. Motivated by the offline carrier
pattern, an obvious remedy is to train on that channel and transfer what it knows to a
deployable model. This is legitimate: complete trajectories of the training cells are available,
so the constraint binds only at inference on the target cell (learning under privileged
information [34]), and we tested it: a teacher using the life-fraction carrier supplied out-of-
fold soft targets that were blended into an online-computable student’s regression objective.
The tested blending scheme did not meet its improvement criterion. The student improved
on only 3 of 11 units, the median change was +7.3% at a blending weight of 0.5, and
degradation grew monotonically with that weight (+2.6%, +7.3%, +13.9% at 0.3, 0.5
and 0.7, Figure 4c). Per-unit Mann–Whitney tests with Holm correction leave one unit
significantly better and none significantly worse, so the accurate reading is that this scheme
did not meet its improvement criterion; neither universal transfer failure nor significant
harm is established. One pattern is consistent with Table 2: the offline cycle convention is
not a universal upper bound: it is better than the fixed-scale convention on 5 of 11 units,
but exceeds the 11.3% noise floor on only 4 (XJTU-2C, XJTU-R2.5, TJU-3 and HUST), and
is worse on the other 6. These are legacy convention diagnostics, not corrected-online
comparisons, and do not identify the reason target blending fails. Whether a different
transfer mechanism succeeds where target blending fails is open, and identifying the failure
mechanism would require a different probe.
Statistical power at the unit level. Cell-level generalization is limited by the two or
three held-out cells in each XJTU batch and TJU-3. The legacy per-unit pairing effect-to-
noise ratios span 0.5–11.9, and six of eleven units reach p ≤0.1; the pooled randomization
result concerns an average across units. These pairing tests differ from the run-level
comparison against the published baseline: in the latter, two units per backbone are non-
significant, with three distinct units in their union (TJU-2 for both, XJTU-RW for TabPFN,
TJU-1 for GBDT). Non-significance does not establish equivalence.
Scope of the data and of the claim. All libraries are laboratory ageing data acquired
under controlled conditions; field data with dynamic loads, temperature excursions, and
calendar ageing is not covered. All claims are scoped to battery SOH estimation, where the
benchmark target is cycle capacity divided by nominal capacity; we make no claim about
degradation tasks whose labels lack that structure.

5. Conclusions
What a feature is compared against (other cells, a recent past, or the earliest retained
record) is a design decision that has been made implicitly inside normalization choices,
and it materially affects error under the tested legacy protocol: replacing a single column’s
convention moves error from −49.6% to +107.8% across eleven evaluation units. The
benchmark SOH label uses nominal capacity as its denominator, while the prevailing
per-cell convention depends on full-lifetime extrema that a cell in service cannot know. We
made the comparison explicit and online-computable as a self-referenced lift.
Under the offline author protocol, off-the-shelf learners with the full history stack are
significantly more accurate than the physics-informed network on 9 of 11 units for each
of two backbones, with none significantly worse. Under the corrected strict-online reader,
compact SRL instead changes median per-cell RMSE by +4.3% for gradient boosting (5
of 11 improve) and +10.4% for the frozen foundation model (3 of 11 improve); feature
computability does not translate into an overall accuracy gain. The companion benchmark
improves on five published deep baselines in 6 of 6 batches with per-batch selection (5 of
6 with a fixed configuration), but its normalization already supplies an implicit comparison.
The unseen-library 22–33% improvement and the randomized pairing results (p = 0.0099
for gradient boosting, p = 0.0476 for the frozen foundation model) are legacy diagnostics
under their stated readers. Tree-model carrier attribution is consistent with HUST and
TJU-3 but has counterexamples and does not establish a universal mechanism.
For sustainable power and energy systems the practical message is economical rather
than architectural. The measured CPU workloads have seconds-per-unit cost without
training on the target system; the SRL feature contract additionally requires each cell’s
earliest record to be retained, with the cost profiles of the two backbones reported as
separate measurements of different workloads and neither presented as an embedded-
deployment claim. Three qualifications travel with that statement. The deployability result
is negative in our corrected evaluation: with target-future and label information removed
from the reader, the reference families do not meet the stated cross-unit improvement
criterion, so what survives is the feature contract rather than an accuracy claim. Every
accuracy figure is obtained on laboratory ageing data under controlled protocols, so what is
established is that each required quantity is computable at time t in service, not that the same
accuracy transfers to field BMS conditions with dynamic loads, calendar ageing and sensor
drift; extending the analysis to such field data, and to fleet-level deployment where per-cell
origin records and cross-fleet heterogeneity interact, is left to future work. The whole
account also presumes a retained commissioning record: without it, the reference cannot
be reconstructed after the fact. We recommend that the comparison target be reported as a
standard ablation in degradation modelling, that evaluation protocols distinguish channels
available offline from those that survive deployment, and that commissioning records be
treated as a functional part of the health-estimation pipeline.
