Longitudinal proxy pack benchmark generation for 
remaining useful life assessment of light electric vehicle 
battery packs in swapping systems
Lu Zhu · Wangwang Zheng · Fengmin M. Zhang
Received: 23 July 2026 / Accepted: 25 September 2026
© The Author(s) 2026
Abstract
Battery swapping enables rapid energy replenishment for light electric vehicles (LEVs), but high-turnover pack 
circulation requires reliable pack-level health and remaining useful life (RUL) assessment. Publicly reusable 
longitudinal ageing datasets for complete LEV packs remain scarce. We develop a proxy-pack data-generation 
and learning framework that organizes independently tested cell-ageing records into fixed-member trajectories 
representing a 48 V-class 16-series configuration. Each trajectory retains 16 physical-cell identities over their 
common-cycle sequence. Cycle-level member states are aggregated into pack features for predicting a conser -
vative minimum-member proxy RUL, with test barcodes isolated before combinatorial augmentation. Across 
ten barcode-level splits in each of three source cohorts, same-source tests with endpoints within the augmented 
training lifetime range achieved a median R² of 0.909 and a median Spearman correlation of 0.997. Tests 
beyond this range retained strong degradation ordering but showed larger absolute errors. A secondary mixed-
source audit of 80 held-out trajectories achieved a median R² of 0.714 and a median Spearman correlation 
of 0.992. A matched composition-control analysis reduced the median R² by only 0.015 after balancing bar -
code reuse and limiting composition overlap, indicating limited sensitivity to training-composition sampling. A 
complementary physical audit using one fixed 12S3P pack confirmed cell-to-branch-to-pack electrical closure 
and revealed persistent voltage limitation and migration of the maximum-temperature location. Proxy-pack 
generation therefore provides a reproducible approach for constructing longitudinal member-aware benchmarks 
from cell-ageing records and evaluating pack-health and RUL models across varied compositions and lifetime 
conditions.

Introduction
 Light electric vehicles (LEVs), including electric two-wheelers and other low-voltage mobility platforms, sup -
port urban commuting, delivery services, and frequent short-distance travel. Compared with plug-in charging, 
battery swapping shortens replenishment time and turns energy provision into a managed process of pack circu-
lation, centralized charging, and station-side screening 1–3. In this setting, a swappable pack must be evaluated 
beyond state of charge. Its operational value also depends on state of health (SOH), remaining useful life (RUL), 
and suitability for future dispatch4–6.
A central obstacle is data availability. Practical LEV swapping systems must balance low cost with limited 
sensing and communication under high pack turnover. Station-side or edge systems may have access only to esti-
mated internal resistance, temperature extrema, charge time, capacity summaries, or other cycle-level descriptors. 
By contrast, pack-level lifetime labels are slow and expensive to obtain. Industrial pack records are also difficult 
to release because of commercial specifications, product identifiers, operating traces, and data-authorization con-
straints. Existing public vehicle-level and accelerated life-testing datasets provide useful references 7,8. A recent 
pack-cycling dataset further provides member-cell voltages, branch currents, thermal measurements, and pack-
level signals under vehicle-oriented dynamic profiles 9. However, these datasets differ from low-voltage LEV 
swapping packs in their fields, protocols, configurations, application domains, or label definitions. Publicly reus-
able full-life LEV pack benchmarks that jointly provide member-cell information and pack-level health or RUL 
labels therefore remain scarce.
Cell-level SOH estimation and RUL prediction have advanced through experimental characterization, 
equivalent-circuit modelling, electrochemical modelling, data-driven learning, and physics-informed hybrid 
approaches4–6. Among public benchmarks, the MIT–Stanford fast-charging dataset is widely used for early-cycle 
lifetime prediction10. Subsequent studies have used this and related datasets to investigate data-efficient progno-
sis, semi-supervised learning, degradation-stage detection, uncertainty-aware prediction, and broader data-driven 
health estimation11–13. These studies show that cell-level cycling records contain strong degradation information 
before severe capacity fade becomes visible. Their data organization nevertheless remains cell-centric: one cell, 
one trajectory, and one lifetime label. This structure supports cell-level SOH and RUL prediction but does not 
directly define a pack-level learning target.
At pack level, health is not governed by an average cell alone. Cell-to-cell inconsistency, weakest-member 
limits, resistance and temperature dispersion, and state-of-charge imbalance can constrain usable energy, power 
capability, and ageing trajectories. New-pack assembly therefore generally seeks cells of the same specification 
and a common production or procurement cohort, followed by screening or matching of capacity, resistance, 
and other initial properties to reduce avoidable heterogeneity14,15. A common cohort nevertheless retains natural 
cell-to-cell variation. Existing work has investigated inconsistency-aware SOH estimation, pack-level SOH pre-
diction under variable configurations, generalized series-pack prognostics, experiment-free inconsistency mod -
elling, and cell-to-pack degradation analysis 16–21. These studies establish that pack-level assessment requires 
explicit aggregation rules and representations of intra-pack heterogeneity. Many approaches, however, depend 
on physical pack experiments, richer sensing, predefined structures, or assumptions that are difficult to reproduce 
from public cell-level data alone.
A related question is whether member-aware health models remain reliable across source cohorts and operating 
domains. Battery health models are sensitive to differences among cells, ageing protocols, operating conditions, 
and target domains. Transfer learning, online correction, multi-domain adaptation, and limited-label calibration 
have therefore been proposed to improve robustness22–26. Few studies, however, examine the intermediate setting 
considered here: a member-aware learning object constructed from public cell records and evaluated under fixed-
composition longitudinal holdout and cross-cohort stress tests.
To address this gap, we develop a longitudinal proxy-pack benchmark generation and learning framework 
for member-aware LEV battery assessment. The primary benchmark assigns 16 distinct cells from a common

Three contributions distinguish this study from conventional cell-level RUL modelling. First, the framework 
converts public cell-ageing records into reproducible longitudinal proxy-pack benchmarks that retain both mem-
ber histories and pack-level aggregates. Second, it evaluates member-aware RUL prediction under barcode-dis -
joint holdout and systematically examines endpoint coverage, composition sampling, feature and target choices, 
series-member count, and cross-cohort shift. Third, the measured 12S3P analysis provides a separate physical 
audit of cell-to-branch-to-pack aggregation and limiting-member dynamics. Although RUL prediction is the pri-
mary demonstration, the retained member-level structure also provides a basis for future studies of pack SOH, 
intra-pack dispersion, weak-member identification, abnormal compositions, and alternative maintenance-oriented 
targets. Together, these elements establish an auditable data-generation and evaluation framework for developing 
member-aware LEV battery-pack diagnostics.
Problem formulation
Light electric vehicle (LEV) swapping stations circulate, charge, grade, dispatch, and retire complete battery 
packs. However, public longitudinal ageing datasets that jointly resolve pack-level outputs and individual mem-
ber states remain scarce. Independently tested cell datasets, by contrast, contain substantially more complete 
ageing trajectories. New battery packs are generally assembled from nominally comparable cells selected from a 
common production or procurement cohort, often with additional screening for capacity, resistance, and other ini-
tial properties14,15. We therefore investigate whether the natural cell-to-cell variation within a public source cohort 
can be organized into fixed-member longitudinal proxy packs for member-aggregated remaining-life assessment. 
Here, a public source cohort is an experimental grouping and is not assumed to be a verified manufacturing lot.
This study focuses on compact all-series LEV packs containing tens of cells. A 48 V-class 16-series (16 S) con-
figuration serves as the primary case, while 13–20 S configurations are examined in sensitivity analyses. Under 
the ideal all-series condition, the same current passes through every member cell, and the pack terminal voltage 
equals the sum of the member-cell voltages. These circuit identities provide a defined electrical basis for member 
aggregation without introducing current allocation among parallel branches. They do not reproduce synchronized 
pack trajectories, shared thermal fields, balancing actions, protection events, or electrically coupled degradation. 
Battery systems containing hundreds or thousands of cells would be better represented using hierarchical cell-to-
module-to-pack constructions rather than unchanged flat member aggregation.

audit provide an intermediate evidence base between public cell-ageing records and longitudinal validation on 
multiple physical LEV packs.
Methods
Source cell cohorts and preprocessing
The source corpus comprises 138 physical cell barcodes and 108,995 usable cycle records extracted from the 
MIT–Stanford raw .mat files. The three public source cohorts contain 46, 48, and 44 cells, respectively, follow -
ing the experimental grouping provided with the dataset. Each row represents one barcode at one valid cycle and 
records the cycle index, discharge capacity, internal resistance, temperature summaries, charge time, and derived 
health quantities. Records are retained if they occur after initialization, their cycle-summary fields are not all zero, 
their reference capacity and SOH are defined, and their RUL is non-negative. All evaluation partitions are defined 
at the physical-cell level using barcode identity. Within each evaluation fold, a barcode is assigned to only one 
data subset.

Proxy pack construction
Same-source fixed-member longitudinal trajectories
Within each predefined barcode split, the primary protocol operates within one public source cohort. Sixteen 
distinct barcodes are assigned once to a fixed virtual 16 S test pack and excluded from the cohort-specific training 
pool. The same 16 members are retained over all cycle indices observed in common. The remaining 28–32 cells 
are used to generate 32 distinct fixed-member training compositions, each of which is followed over its common 
observed cycle range. No interpolation, carry-forward imputation, or lifetime-ratio alignment is applied. Obser -
vations are sampled at five-cycle intervals, and each trajectory receives equal total training weight. Training cells 
may recur across compositions, whereas test barcodes remain excluded from every training trajectory.
Reuse of training cells expands the range of member compositions available for model development. The 
dependence introduced by this augmentation is quantified using the number of unique compositions, pairwise 
barcode-overlap distributions, and barcode-use distributions. A matched composition-control analysis addition -
ally balances barcode use and limits pairwise overlap to 12 members, as described in Sect.  3.5. Trajectories that 
share source-cell barcodes are treated as correlated augmentation samples.
Supporting proxy constructions
Two supporting constructions test robustness beyond the primary same-source longitudinal setting. The mixed-
source construction broadens the composition range while retaining member identity and common-cycle order. 
Cross-sectional constructions test whether predictive patterns persist when member observations are recombined 
at individual degradation stages, with and without lifetime-aligned pairing.
In the lifetime-aligned same-stage construction, member observations are combined at individual degradation 
stages rather than retained as fixed-member histories. Normalized cycle position, defined as cycle index divided 
by eventual complete cycle life, is divided into 100 bins. Starting from an anchor bin, the candidate search 
expands by at most ten bins. The nearest observation from each eligible barcode is retained, and 16 members are 
sampled from a candidate pool containing at most 64 observations. Eventual cell lifetime is therefore used for 
member pairing, although cycle-ratio features are excluded from model inputs.
The random-stage construction provides a lifetime-independent alternative. It samples 16 distinct barcodes and 
one observed cycle from each selected barcode without using eventual lifetime for pairing. Barcode identities 
remain isolated across the training, validation, and test partitions in both cross-sectional constructions. Because 
individual barcodes may recur across derived instances within a partition, these rows are treated as correlated 
augmentation samples.

Learning models and analytical tests
Two tabular regression models are evaluated on the MIT–Stanford-derived proxy-pack benchmark. Ridge 
regression is fitted after standardization using the training-data means and standard deviations. Its regulariza -
tion parameter is selected from 0.1, 1, 10, and 100. The Random Forest search considers 200 or 400 trees, no 
maximum-depth constraint or maximum depths of 8 or 16, and minimum leaf sizes of 1 or 5. Hyperparameters 
are selected by validation-set MAE in the barcode-disjoint cross-sectional development experiment; test data are 
not used. The selected 27-feature Random Forest model uses 400 trees, a maximum depth of 16, and a minimum 
leaf size of 1. These values are fixed across all longitudinal experiments, repeated splits, and sensitivity analyses, 
with no split-specific retuning. Performance is evaluated using MAE (cycles), RMSE (cycles), Spearman rank 
correlation, and the coefficient of determination (R²).
Grouped ablation and joint permutation analyses quantify the predictive information contributed by capacity 
state (discharge capacity and SOH), internal resistance, temperature, and charge time. In the permutation analy -
sis, all columns within a feature group are permuted together using a common row permutation. This procedure 
preserves dependence among features within the group while breaking their association with the target. All fea -
ture comparisons use the same locked model and matched barcode partitions.
Member-count sensitivity is evaluated using 13, 14, 16, and 20 distinct cells under the random-stage all-series 
proxy definition. This analysis changes only the number of aggregated series members and does not represent 
parallel current sharing. Separately, fixed-member trajectories are used to track the cells with the lowest SOH, 
lowest discharge capacity, and highest internal resistance. These identities are also calculated using causal trailing 
25-cycle medians, with at least five observations required, to reduce sensitivity to cycle-level fluctuations. Each 
estimate uses only information available at the current and preceding cycles.
Evaluation and sensitivity analyses
The primary evaluation uses same-source fixed-member longitudinal trajectories. For each source cohort, 16 cell 
barcodes are assigned to one fixed test trajectory and excluded from the cells used for training. The remaining 
cells are recombined into 32 augmented training trajectories. This procedure is repeated across ten barcode-level 
splits for each of the three source cohorts, producing 30 cohort-by-split evaluations.
Endpoint-support analysis compares the endpoint of each held-out trajectory with the endpoints represented by 
its 32 augmented training trajectories. A held-out endpoint is classified as covered when it falls between the mini-
mum and maximum training-trajectory endpoints. This analysis separates interpolation within the represented 
lifetime range from prediction beyond that range.
Composition-control sensitivity repeats the same 30 matched evaluations after balancing barcode use and lim-
iting the overlap between any pair of 16 S training compositions to no more than 12 members. This comparison

A secondary mixed-source evaluation examines a broader range of cell compositions. For each predefined 
regrouping seed, the 138 barcodes are shuffled and the first 128 are partitioned into eight mutually disjoint fixed 
16-member trajectories. The remaining ten barcodes are unused in that regrouping. Each of the eight trajectories 
is held out once, while the other seven are used for training. Ten regroupings therefore produce 80 held-out tra -
jectory evaluations.
The primary mixed-source result retains all selected cells, including the cell with the reported 148-cycle end -
point. A separate sensitivity analysis excludes only the ten held-out trajectories containing that cell. This compari-
son quantifies the influence of the short-lifetime endpoint without replacing the primary all-cell result.
The cross-sectional same-stage and random-stage stress tests use ten predefined barcode-level partitions. Each 
partition contains 97 training, 21 validation, and 20 test cell barcodes. The reported distributions and percentiles 
quantify sensitivity to barcode composition within the finite 138-cell corpus. They are not interpreted as confi -
dence intervals for independent physical battery packs.
Strict leave-one-source-cohort-out evaluation is retained as an out-of-domain stress test rather than the primary 
assembly setting. Target adaptation is evaluated using barcode-disjoint calibration. Calibration and evaluation 
proxies are constructed from non-overlapping target-cell identities. Calibration budgets of 1, 5, 20, or 100 snap-
shots are generated from a separate group of 16 fully labelled target cells.
To characterize observable source-cohort shift at the physical-cell level, the first 100 cycles of each of the 138 
barcodes were summarized using 14 early features: medians and interquartile ranges of discharge capacity, inter-
nal resistance, minimum, mean and maximum temperature, and charge time, together with reference discharge 
capacity and the number of valid early-cycle rows. SOH, feature slopes, and complete cycle life were excluded. 
A standardized multinomial logistic-regression classifier was evaluated using repeated stratified fivefold cross-
validation with ten repeats and compared with a class-prior dummy classifier. Figure 5a reports mean balanced 
accuracy and the 2.5th–97.5th percentile range across the 50 validation folds. For each feature, source-cohort 
effect size was calculated as η², defined as the between-cohort sum of squares divided by the total sum of squares; 
the three largest η² values are highlighted in Fig. 5b. These quantities describe observable cohort and protocol 
signatures and are not interpreted as causal effects.
Statistical comparisons are summarized across matched barcode partitions rather than individual proxy rows 
because cell barcodes recur across derived instances. Model, feature, and construction comparisons are there -
fore reported descriptively across matched partitions. Figure 1 summarizes the analysis workflow and evidence 
hierarchy.
Physical 12S3P electrical closure and member dynamics
A complementary physical analysis uses the public CC–CV/WLTP battery-pack dataset9. The proxy framework 
represents a pack through member states and their aggregate behaviour. This analysis therefore tests electrical 
closure across measured cell, branch, and pack signals and tracks limiting-member identities under coupled 
operation. The dataset records one fixed 12S3P lithium-ion pack over 286 complete and consecutive cycles, span-
ning cycles 125–410. These records comprise 217 WLTP cycles and 69 capacity-check cycles. Available mea -
surements include 36 member-cell voltages, 72 top and bottom cell-surface temperatures, three branch currents, 
three branch voltages, total pack current, and two pack-voltage measurements. The pack is analysed in its native 
12S3P configuration, providing a physical audit of member-aware aggregation rather than a direct evaluation of 
16 S proxy-RUL prediction.
Electrical closure is evaluated at every timestamp using four classes of residual. These comprise total pack 
current minus the sum of the three branch currents, and each branch voltage minus the sum of its twelve cell 
voltages. They also include pack voltage minus the mean branch voltage and the difference between the two pack-
voltage measurements. Pooled quantiles and graphical summaries use up to 1,000 regularly spaced timestamps

most frequently in the corresponding timestamp-level sequence. The dominant fraction and the number of dis -
tinct identities observed within each cycle are also recorded. Identity transitions between consecutive cycles are 
counted separately for the WLTP and capacity-check records.

Lifetime support and mixed-source composition stress test
Retaining member identity over time converts the proxy object from a recombined row into a complete virtual-
pack ageing trajectory. As a broader composition stress test, 128 pooled barcodes formed eight mutually disjoint 
16-member packs per regrouping and each pack was held out once. Across 80 held-out trajectories, the full-
feature Random Forest predicted the minimum-member proxy RUL with median MAE of 57.34 cycles, median 
RMSE of 70.21 cycles, median Spearman correlation of 0.992 and median R² of 0.714. Member-aggregated 
features therefore tracked degradation order consistently, while absolute endpoint prediction varied among held-
out compositions.

Combinatorial augmentation, member-reuse sensitivity and proxy analysis
We next examined whether the longitudinal result depended on how the augmented training compositions were 
sampled and which member-level information carried the predictive signal. The analyses in this section therefore 
progress from composition reuse to cross-sectional augmentation, grouped feature attribution and proxy-defini -
tion sensitivity.
The overlap-and-reuse sensitivity construction reduced dependence on repeatedly selected training cells with-
out changing the central prediction finding. Across the three cohorts, the standard deviation of barcode reuse 
counts decreased from 2.78 to 2.98 in the original unique random compositions to 0.43–0.45 under balanced 
reuse, while all 32 training compositions per split remained unique and maximum pairwise overlap was limited 
to 12 members. Across the 30 matched tests, the median controlled-minus-original change was − 0.0147 for R² 
and + 0.0004 for Spearman correlation. The longitudinal result was therefore robust to substantially more even 
use of the available training cells.
The cross-sectional random-stage construction provides a stricter combinatorial stress test because eventual 
complete lifetime is not used to pair members. Across ten barcode-level splits, it achieved median R² of 0.800 
(2.5th–97.5th percentile, 0.548–0.848) and median Spearman correlation of 0.922 (0.829–0.967). The submitted 
same-stage split reproduced R² = 0.9362 and Spearman = 0.9875, but repeated same-stage R² ranged from − 2.279 
to 0.974 and was strongly associated with test-target composition. The random-stage result is therefore used as 
the central cross-sectional estimate.
Feature attribution shows that the signal is concentrated rather than broadly multiphysical. The 27 fitted col -
umns contain no cycle-life, cycle-ratio, RUL or target field. Nevertheless, discharge capacity and SOH are observ-
able ageing proxies: removing these eight capacity-state features reduced median random-stage R² from 0.800 to 
0.424, whereas the eight features alone achieved 0.764. Joint permutation caused a median R² loss of 1.352 for 
capacity state, compared with 0.032 for resistance, 0.005 for charge time and approximately zero for temperature. 
The model is consequently interpreted as capacity-state-dominated member aggregation.
Within the all-series proxy definition, changing the member count from 13 to 14, 16 or 20 produced similar 
median minimum-label R² values of 0.787, 0.789, 0.800 and 0.778. At 16 members, replacing the minimum target 
with the lower decile or mean reduced median R² to 0.743 and 0.577, and minimum-versus-mean target concor-
dance was only 0.259. These results support limited robustness to series-member count within the defined proxy 
object while confirming that performance remains dependent on the chosen pack-level target.

Discussion
The central result is that same-source fixed-member proxy trajectories retain sufficient longitudinal information 
for member-aggregated RUL learning across held-out compositions. This setting reflects the practical assembly 
of nominally comparable cells while preserving natural variation in capacity, resistance and degradation rate. 
High rank consistency across all three source cohorts shows that the aggregated observations contain a stable 
degradation-order signal. Absolute RUL is more demanding because the held-out endpoint must be represented 
by the augmented training compositions; this explains why strong Spearman correlation can coexist with poor or 
negative R². Mixed-source holdout and cross-cohort transfer extend this test beyond the primary assembly setting 
and expose additional distribution shift, but they are not required to justify the same-source use case.
Combinatorial member reuse broadens the range of training compositions available from a finite cell corpus. 
The matched sensitivity analysis showed that balancing barcode use and reducing composition overlap did not 
materially change rank consistency or absolute RUL performance, indicating that the longitudinal result was not 
driven by a small set of frequently selected cells. Because feasible overlap depends on source pool size, gener -
ated benchmarks should report composition uniqueness, pairwise overlap and per-barcode use rather than adopt a 
universal threshold. Barcode isolation between training and test members remains the essential safeguard against 
identity leakage.
The generated object is not limited to the RUL regression reported here. Compared with pack-only benchmarks 
that do not expose member-cell states, each trajectory retains member identity and cycle summaries alongside 
pack aggregates. The same structure can therefore support future pack-SOH, inconsistency-evolution, weak-
member, abnormal-composition, and alternative-target studies. These are enabled uses of the data structure rather 
than additional tasks validated here.
The feature and target analyses clarify what the proxy model learns. Capacity-state descriptors carried most 
of the predictive signal, while resistance, temperature, and charge-time summaries provided smaller incremental 
contributions in this corpus. The minimum-member target is a conservative series-system label, but its disagree-
ment with lower-decile and mean targets shows that it is not interchangeable with every physical retirement crite-
rion14–15,17–21,27–28. The 12S3P reconstruction complements this statistical evidence by demonstrating current and 
voltage closure, parallel-current imbalance, and longitudinal member dynamics 9,29–31. Multiple fixed physical 
LEV packs with complete ageing endpoints are still required to evaluate absolute deployment-level RUL.
The scope is also size-dependent. Member-wise construction is practical for compact LEV configurations with 
tens of cells, for which individual members and their dispersion can be represented directly. Packs containing 
hundreds or thousands of cells introduce module hierarchy, parallel current sharing, interconnect variation, and 
spatial thermal gradients. Extension to those systems should therefore use hierarchical, topology-aware module 
aggregation rather than merely increasing the member count.
The retained 148-cycle cell illustrates why an extreme short-lifetime member should not be removed solely 
because it degrades average model performance. In a physical pack, early degradation or abrupt cell-level anoma-
lies would motivate BMS-assisted monitoring of member voltage, temperature and protection events, followed 
by decisions such as continued service, derating, repair, repurposing or recycling. The present independently 
tested-cell records demonstrate a short lifetime rather than an abrupt in-pack failure, and they contain no BMS 
event stream. Accordingly, this case supports a lifetime-coverage stress test, not a claim that the proxy model has 
already implemented physical fault diagnosis or intervention decisions.
Conclusions
This study develops fixed-member longitudinal proxy-pack trajectories as a reproducible bridge from cell-ageing 
records to member-aware pack-level remaining-life assessment. In the same-source experiments, degradation 
ordering remained strong across all three public source cohorts, and tests within the augmented training endpoint

Retained member histories also enable future extension to other member-aware targets. The present flat member-

development from sparse pack-level evidence. Validation on multiple fixed physical LEV packs with complete
