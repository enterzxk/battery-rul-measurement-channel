# A Post-Mortem of a False Positive: How a Fitting Window and a Label Unit Manufactured an 8.6% RUL Gain

> Draft v2.0 — 2026-11-07. Supersedes v1.0. Every number traceable to docs/e1_v2_fixed_results.md, docs/autonomous_research/AUDIT_AGENT3.md, docs/autonomous_research/B_SECTION_FALSIFIED.md.

## Abstract

We report the failure of a positive result in battery remaining-useful-life (RUL) prediction and trace it to two mechanically explainable artifacts. In an earlier round we reported that adding two-RC parameters fitted to short pulse relaxation improved cross-condition RUL error by 8.6% (cell-macro MAE 6.587 to 6.020, 95% CI [-0.999, -0.133], leave-one-condition-out) on 64 LFP cells across 11 operating conditions. Two defects were found on audit. First, the relaxation fit window began at the current-carrying sample, so the model had to bridge a 52.3% voltage step with a decaying exponential; the fast time constant collapsed to its lower bound in 100% of 17,130 traces, contributing six constant columns, and the associated amplitude became a step-bridging term rather than a physical polarisation. Second, the prediction target was the number of remaining reference tests, not cycles; the per-interval cycle count had been read as a cumulative counter, and because the inspection pace varies by a factor of 1.98 across cells, an error measured in inspections implicitly weights cells unequally. After repairing the window, fitting in seconds, and expressing the target in reconstructed cumulative cycles, the increment falls to 5.3% and its interval crosses zero under both cell-level ([-80.77, +10.45] cycles) and condition-cluster ([-115.02, +39.07] cycles) resampling, with 6 of 11 conditions improving. The physical parameterisation no longer beats raw waveform descriptors (6.238 vs 6.155). What survives is that pulse features do carry degradation information (6.587 versus 13.501 for inspection index alone and 9.148 for state of health alone) but do not reliably improve on a discharge curve. A pre-registered test of the natural explanation - that identifiability limits cross-condition stability - was falsified (Spearman +0.375, p = 0.286). We also show that with 11 condition groups the design has insufficient power to detect the effect size in question (about 49 groups would be needed), which means no re-analysis of this dataset could have produced a defensible positive claim.

## 1 Introduction

A negative result is only useful if its mechanism is identified. This paper reports one: a positive RUL result that we ourselves published internally, the two implementation defects that produced it, and the diagnostic path that exposed them.

The scientific question was whether non-cyclic measurements - pulse relaxation in particular - carry information that cyclic curves do not, and how much that information is worth for remaining-life prediction under a leave-one-condition-out protocol. That question remains open and important: a recent critical review lists impedance and relaxation descriptors among the observable channels but gives no quantitative contribution to RUL, and the closest published channel ablation we are aware of targets state of health rather than RUL and uses random rather than condition-held-out splits.

What we report here is not an answer to that question. It is the demonstration that our first answer was an artifact, the identification of the two mechanisms, and a statement of what can still be claimed.

The two mechanisms are ordinary. The first is a window boundary: a relaxation fit that includes the sample at which the current is still flowing must represent a voltage step of about half the total excursion with a decaying exponential, and the optimiser satisfies this by driving the fast time constant to its lower bound. The second is a unit: a target counted in reference tests is not a target counted in cycles, and when the number of cycles per test varies by a factor of two across cells, error measured in tests is not a scale-invariant statistic.

Neither is exotic, and both are invisible in the usual report. The first produces a converged fit with a plausible-looking parameter vector; the second produces a familiar mean-absolute-error number. Together they produced a result that passed our own pre-registered criterion.

The paper is organised as a post-mortem: Section 2 states the original claim and its protocol, Section 3 and 4 dissect the two artifacts, Section 5 reports the repaired experiment, Section 6 states what survives, Section 7 reports a falsified mechanism hypothesis, Section 8 quantifies the design's power, and Section 9 extracts recommendations.

## 2 The original claim and its protocol

Data: 64 LFP cells in 11 operating-condition groups, each characterised at up to 77 reference performance tests providing a 100 s slow pulse at 20/50/90% state of charge in both directions (101 samples) and a constant-current discharge curve. Target: the number of remaining reference tests before the first observation with state of health at or below 80%. Model: one gradient-boosted regressor held fixed across all feature sets. Protocol: leave-one-condition-out over 11 folds, metric cell-macro mean absolute error, uncertainty from a 5,000-resample cell-level paired bootstrap.

Under that protocol, adding two-RC relaxation parameters to a 54-dimensional discharge-curve baseline reduced error from 6.587 to 6.020, an 8.6% reduction with a 95% interval of [-0.999, -0.133] and 62.5% of cells improved. The fitted parameters also beat unfitted waveform descriptors (6.020 versus 6.155), which we read as evidence that the physical representation rather than the extra dimensions carried the gain.

Both readings were wrong, for two independent reasons.

## 3 Artifact 1: the fitting window

The relaxation model was v(t) = c + a1 exp(-t/tau1) + a2 exp(-t/tau2), fitted to the samples after the extremum of the pulse, with t in sample indices. The window began at the extremum itself - the sample at which the current is still flowing. Between that sample and the next, the voltage moves by a median 52.3% of the entire relaxation excursion. A two-exponential model cannot represent a discontinuity, so the optimiser used the fastest available exponential to bridge it.

The consequence is visible in the fitted values. Across all 17,130 traces, tau1 took the value 1.000000 or 1.000001 with a standard deviation below 1.4e-8 - it was pinned to the lower bound in 100% of traces. Six of the 30 feature columns were therefore constant, and the associated amplitude a1 described a step, not a polarisation process.

The repair is to begin the window one sample later, to express time in seconds, and to bound the time constants by the relaxation window (60 s). After repair, all 17,130 traces converge, tau1 spans 1.08-3.79 s with median 2.13 s and no boundary hits, and tau2 spans 15.2-34.1 s with median 21.1 s. The two-exponential form remains necessary: it fits substantially better than one exponential.

The repaired parameters carry less predictive value than the broken ones. With the corrected fit, the discharge curve plus relaxation parameters reaches 6.238 on the original target, versus 6.155 for discharge curve plus unfitted waveform descriptors. The physical parameterisation is now worse than the descriptors, not better. The apparent advantage of the physical representation was a property of a degenerate fit.

This is consistent with an independent published ablation on state of health, where an equivalent-circuit parameterisation also underperformed the raw spectral input. We note the agreement without claiming that our result establishes a general principle.

## 4 Artifact 2: the label unit

The dataset provides a per-interval cycle count. We initially treated it as a cumulative counter, found it non-monotone in the inspection index, and abandoned it in favour of the inspection index as the time axis. That reading was wrong: the field is the number of cycles between consecutive reference tests, not a running total. Four independent checks establish this - it is zero at the first test, the next values fall as often as they rise, the median absolute correlation with the inspection index is 0.611 with no cell exceeding 0.8, and its cumulative sum spans about 4,000 cycles, consistent with roughly 100 cycles per test.

Cumulating it produces a clean aging clock: the Spearman correlation between state of health and cumulative cycles is exactly -1.0000 in 52 of 64 cells. End of life falls at 2,308-8,303 cumulative cycles (median 3,870).

The two clocks are related but not interchangeable. Correlation between end-of-life inspection index and end-of-life cumulative cycles is +0.811, but the pace - cycles per inspection - ranges from 67.6 to 133.7, a factor of 1.98. A model that predicts remaining inspections is therefore predicting a cell-specifically rescaled version of remaining cycles, and a mean absolute error in inspection units implicitly weights cells by their inspection pace. The statistic is not scale-invariant, and it cannot be compared with literature that reports cycles.

## 5 The repaired experiment

With the corrected fit and the cumulative-cycle target, on the same queries, splits, model and bootstrap:

| Feature set | Inspection units | Cycles |
|---|---:|---:|
| Discharge curve | 6.587 | 729.8 |
| + relaxation parameters (repaired) | 6.238 | 694.1 |
| + waveform descriptors | 6.155 | 696.8 |
| + capacity and state of health | 6.793 | 696.8 |
| Relaxation parameters only | 8.596 | 832.1 |
| State of health only | 9.148 | 859.2 |
| Inspection index only | 13.501 | 1154.3 |

The headline contrast on the cycle target is a reduction of 12.11 cycles. Its 95% interval is [-80.77, +10.45] under cell-level resampling and [-115.02, +39.07] under condition-cluster resampling; 6 of 11 conditions improve. The effect is not distinguishable from zero under either unit of inference.

We also recomputed the intra-class correlation for the outcome: 0.414, a design effect of 2.99 and an effective sample size of 21.4 rather than 64. Cell-level bootstrap therefore understates the standard error by about a factor of 1.7, which is what the two intervals above reflect.

## 6 What survives

Three statements remain supported.

First, pulse features do carry degradation information. The discharge curve alone reaches 6.587, against 13.501 for the inspection index alone and 9.148 for state of health alone. A model that had learned only the inspection schedule could not do this.

Second, adding capacity and state of health makes the model worse, not better (6.793 versus 6.587), under both units. This is a robust negative interaction and argues against a reading in which pulse features are merely a capacity proxy.

Third, the channel does not reliably improve on a discharge curve once the fit and the unit are repaired. The honest claim is that when a full reference test is available, pulse relaxation adds nothing measurable; we cannot speak to the case where it is unavailable, which is the practically interesting one.

## 7 A falsified mechanism hypothesis

The natural explanation for why some relaxation parameters change direction across operating conditions is identifiability: a time constant that a short window constrains poorly should be unstable. We pre-registered a test of this and it failed. The failure supports two claims of different strength, and we state them separately.

**We did not observe the pre-registered monotonic relationship.** Across 5,710 corrected UConn fits over 11 conditions (W = 60 s) and 1,649 Bayreuth fits over 23 conditions (W = 10,800 s) we obtain ten (dataset, parameter) points, each with a dimensionless ratio tau/W and a sign consistency. The relationship is not monotone decreasing; the rank correlation is +0.375. This test cannot establish absence: ten points with four distinct values of tau/W carry almost no power for a monotonicity claim, and we do not use the p-value as a result.

**Independently of power, a counter-example rejects the mechanism.** The hypothesis is directional - sign consistency should be a monotone decreasing function of identifiability - and a directional claim is refuted by a single well-determined contrary point. UConn's slow polarisation amplitude and slow time constant sit at tau/W = 0.345, among the least identifiable settings in the table, and their sign consistency is 1.000: the same direction in every one of the 11 conditions. Whatever governs cross-condition sign stability here, it is not how well the window constrains the parameter.

| Dataset | Parameter | tau/W | Sign consistency | Conditions |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bayreuth | fast amplitude | 0.00000 | 0.826 | 23 |
| Bayreuth | fast time constant | 0.00000 | 0.696 | 23 |
| Bayreuth | slow amplitude | 0.00003 | 0.609 | 23 |
| Bayreuth | slow time constant | 0.00003 | 0.609 | 23 |
| UConn | fast amplitude | 0.03442 | 0.636 | 11 |
| UConn | fast time constant | 0.03442 | 0.545 | 11 |
| UConn | slow amplitude | 0.34541 | **1.000** | 11 |
| UConn | slow time constant | 0.34541 | **1.000** | 11 |
| UConn | steady-state voltage | 1.00000 | 1.000 | 11 |
| Bayreuth | steady-state voltage | 1.00000 | 0.652 | 23 |

Two caveats. The two datasets' relaxation windows differ by a factor of 180, so their nominal two-RC parameters describe different physical processes - UConn's slow time constant (21 s) is shorter than Bayreuth's fast one (60 s) - and tau/W is the only comparable quantity. And sign consistency measures reproducibility, not predictive value: a parameter can be perfectly consistent across conditions and carry no information, as Section 5 already showed for the fast amplitude.

The practical consequence is narrow and worth stating plainly: fit quality is not evidence of transferability, and neither is identifiability. Both were natural inferences and both are wrong here.

## 8 Expressing the uncertainty that remains

The repaired experiment still produces a point estimate from a fit whose parameters are only partly constrained, on a target that is itself interval-censored: end of life is observed only at reference-test granularity, so each label is a bracket rather than a number. We therefore do not claim a gain; we claim that the uncertainty should be carried explicitly rather than hidden behind a mean absolute error.

Two devices are used, and neither is a source of improvement. First, each relaxation parameter is represented by its profile interval rather than its point estimate, so that a poorly constrained parameter contributes an interval of features rather than a spurious value. Second, the regression is trained with an interval-censored likelihood, treating each end-of-life observation as the bracket implied by the inspection grid.

We report this as a reporting convention, not a method contribution. Section 7 has just shown that identifiability does not explain the cross-condition instability we observe; it would be inconsistent to now argue that modelling identifiability improves prediction. The interval representation is justified by the fact that the uncertainty exists, not by any claim that handling it helps.

## 9 Why the design could not have succeeded

With 11 condition groups, a paired design, an effect size near 0.4 and 80% power at alpha = 0.05, roughly 49 groups would be required. The available dataset provides 11. This is a property of the data, not of the method: no re-analysis of these 64 cells can produce a defensible condition-level claim about an effect of this size. It also means the earlier cell-level interval, which excluded zero, was significant only because the unit of inference did not match the unit of generalisation.

## 10 Recommendations

Four practices would have caught both artifacts before any conclusion was drawn.

Report the fitted parameters, not only the model error. A time constant pinned to its bound is visible in one histogram and invisible in an error table.

Fit with an explicit time axis in physical units, and set parameter bounds from the observation window rather than from a generic plausible range.

State the unit of the target and its relation to the deployment quantity. If a proxy unit is used, report the per-unit scaling and its spread across the population.

Match the unit of inference to the unit of generalisation. If the claim is about unseen operating conditions, the resampling unit must be the condition.

To these we add a fifth, learned at our own expense: a pre-registration is only as good as its power to test what it pre-registers. A monotonicity criterion evaluated on four distinct values of the independent variable is not a test, and committing to it in advance did not make it one.

## 11 Limitations

The repaired experiment still rests on a single dataset of 64 cells across 11 conditions, and Section 9 shows it is underpowered for the effect in question. The sampling interval of 1 s is inferred from external documentation rather than from the distributed files, which contain no time column; a factor-of-two error would rescale the time constants without changing any comparative conclusion. The second dataset supports only the identifiability test, not RUL prediction, because its cells fade by at most 10.4% and never approach the end-of-life criterion. The falsified mechanism test has low power for the monotonic trend and should not be read as evidence of absence; the counter-example in Section 7, by contrast, does not depend on power.
