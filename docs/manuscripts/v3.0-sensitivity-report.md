# Donor Count and Calibrator Choice at a Fixed Label Budget: An Exploratory Study of Cross-Condition Battery RUL Calibration

> Draft v3.0 — 2026-11-08. Negative/sensitivity report. Every number traceable to `.research/exp/EXP-001..011`.

## Abstract

We set out to establish a *donor-identity floor* in cross-condition battery remaining-useful-life calibration: the hypothesis that, once a single donor cell's trajectory is exhausted, only additional donor cells can reduce error. The floor was not established, and the donor effect did not survive estimator controls. Across 64 LFP cells in 11 operating conditions, adding a second full donor trajectory reduced cell-macro calibration error from 4.654 to 4.365 (Delta = 0.289, 95% CI [0.149, 0.471], 11/11 conditions, both mean and median aggregation). Under a matched row budget of eight labels, the same contrast gave Delta = 0.190 (CI [0.096, 0.305], 11/11, Holm-adjusted p = 0.037). But the effect is confined to affine calibrators: with isotonic calibration it is 0.036 (p = 0.37) and with nearest-neighbour calibration it *reverses* to -0.148 (p = 0.0001, 0/11 conditions improved). After Holm correction across the four calibrators, only the reversal survives. The single-donor row axis did not saturate: adding rows from k = 16 to k = 64 still bought 0.03-0.15 MAE, above the pre-registered 0.05 equivalence margin. We report these as an auditable negative result and a protocol-sensitivity study, and we document three implementation defects in our own analysis that were found by cross-model code review rather than by self-inspection.

## 1 Introduction

Cross-condition evaluation of battery life models is usually reported as a single number: mean absolute error under leave-one-condition-out. That protocol asks the model to predict a condition it has never seen and from which it has no labels. In deployment this is unrealistic, because a newly deployed condition always produces some history first.

Our starting hypothesis was that the supervision budget has two axes - the number of labeled rows k and the number of distinct donor cells m - and that they saturate at different points, with a *floor* on the row axis: once one donor's trajectory is used up, only new donors help. If true, the deployment recommendation would be to acquire additional completed lifetimes rather than more labels from the same one.

The hypothesis failed in two stages. The floor did not appear: rows kept helping beyond the point where the design had power to detect saturation. And the donor effect, which was large and consistent under the pre-registered protocol, turned out to depend on the calibrator: it vanishes under isotonic calibration and reverses under nearest-neighbour calibration.

We report the sequence because the failure is informative and because the implementation defects we made are, we believe, common.

## 2 Protocol

Data: 64 LFP cells in 11 operating-condition groups, characterised at up to 77 reference performance tests providing a 100 s pulse at 20/50/90% state of charge in both directions and a constant-current discharge curve. Target: remaining reference tests to end of life. Predictor: one gradient-boosted regressor on a 54-dimensional discharge-curve baseline, held fixed throughout; all experiments are post-processing of its leave-one-condition-out predictions.

Calibration: an affine map fitted on k labeled rows drawn only from cells *other than* the one being evaluated (strict, cell-disjoint), then applied to the held-out cell. Uncertainty: condition-level paired bootstrap over the 11 conditions, with mean and median aggregation across Monte-Carlo draws both reported.

Protocol lock: four degrees of freedom (aggregation rule, affine scope, donor eligibility, sampling) were frozen before the main experiments, giving a maximum cross-configuration drift of 0.018 MAE against a within-configuration seed standard deviation of 0.0063.

## 3 The floor is not established

Fixing one donor and adding rows within its own trajectory, the change from k = 16 to k = 64 was +0.108 and +0.146 (mean aggregation, two seeds) and +0.033 and +0.074 (median). All four intervals have an upper bound above the pre-registered +-0.05 equivalence margin, so the floor is not established.

The test is also weak for a structural reason: only 9 of 64 cells have at least 64 rows, and they fall in just three conditions, so the paired unit count is three and the interval carries almost no information. We report the result as inconclusive rather than as evidence that rows do not matter, and we note that rows continue to help by 0.03-0.15 over this range.

## 4 The donor effect, and its dependence on the calibrator

With full trajectories (each donor contributing all its rows), error falls from 4.654 (m = 1) to 4.365 (m = 2), Delta = 0.289, CI [0.149, 0.471], 11/11 conditions, both aggregations (median Delta = 0.275). Further donors add 0.090, 0.044 and 0.031, each still positive and significant.

Full-trajectory donor count also increases the number of labeled rows, so this contrast cannot separate donor identity from row count. Under an exact matched row budget of k = 8, splitting eight rows across two donors instead of one gives Delta = 0.190 (CI [0.096, 0.305], 11/11, Holm-adjusted p = 0.037); at k = 16, 0.234 (10/11, Holm 0.082); at k = 32, 0.269 (8/9, Holm 0.064). Only the k = 8 result survives correction.

The estimator control is decisive. Holding the budget at k = 8:

| Calibrator | m = 1 | m = 2 | Delta | p | Conditions improved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| affine | 5.125 | 4.918 | +0.206 | 0.037 | 9/11 |
| clipped affine | 5.113 | 4.908 | +0.204 | 0.020 | 10/11 |
| isotonic | 4.566 | 4.530 | +0.036 | 0.368 | 7/11 |
| nearest neighbour | 5.314 | 5.462 | -0.148 | 0.0001 | 0/11 |

After Holm correction across the four calibrators, only the reversal survives. The donor effect is therefore a property of linear calibration, not of donor identity.

We do not claim that calibrator choice dominates donor count. The comparison (0.559 MAE from switching affine to isotonic at m = 1, versus 0.206 from adding a donor) is descriptive; establishing an interaction would require paired uncertainty on the contrast, and generalising it would require evaluation on held-out data. Selecting isotonic because it performed best here would be selection on the test set.

## 5 Three implementation defects found by cross-model review

Three errors in our own analysis were caught by an independent model reading our code. None was caught by self-inspection.

First, an early label-budget experiment drew a single random sample per configuration and reported it as if it were the expectation; the apparent non-monotonicity in p-values disappeared once 1,000 draws were used. Second, a donor-axis experiment contained no Monte-Carlo loop at all, so the reported mean-versus-median contrast aggregated across evaluated cells rather than across draws and was not the pre-registered sensitivity test. Third, a matched-budget experiment allocated rows as floor(k/m) per donor, so at k = 8 with m = 3 the total was six rows rather than eight; the resulting 'spreading labels too thin hurts' finding was an artifact and reversed once exact allocation was used.

All three share a property: they are invisible in a results table and visible in the code. We recommend that post-processing pipelines of this kind be reviewed by a reader who did not write them.

## 6 What can and cannot be claimed

Can be claimed: under affine calibration at a budget of eight labels, splitting the labels across two donor cells rather than one reduces calibration error by about 0.19 MAE (3.7%), in 11 of 11 conditions, after correction for multiple comparisons. With full trajectories, a second donor reduces error by 0.29 MAE, in 11 of 11 conditions, under both aggregations.

Cannot be claimed: that donor diversity has a general benefit; that the row axis saturates; that there is an optimal donor count; that donor identity is a mechanism; that calibrator choice dominates donor count.

## 7 Limitations

Single dataset, 64 cells, 11 conditions. The condition is the statistical unit, giving n = 11 and a minimum detectable effect of about 0.30 MAE for the main contrast. The k = 32 arm has only nine eligible conditions because it requires donors with at least 32 rows, which is an eligibility confound rather than a row effect. The floor test has three paired units. The second dataset available to us has no end-of-life endpoint and cannot supply RUL labels. All experiments are post-processing of one fixed predictor, so nothing here speaks to how the conclusions change with a different model.

## 8 Conclusion

A supervision-budget effect that looked like a donor-identity floor under the pre-registered protocol did not survive estimator controls. We report the negative result, the controls that produced it, and three implementation defects that an independent reviewer found in our code.
