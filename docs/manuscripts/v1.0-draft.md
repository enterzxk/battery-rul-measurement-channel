# Measuring what cycling cannot see: a controlled comparison of non-cyclic measurement channels for cross-condition battery RUL prediction

> Draft v1.0 — 2026-11-07. All numbers traceable to `docs/e1_final_results.md` (canonical for E1/E2), `docs/tier2_results.md`, `docs/bayreuth_D_results.md`.

## Abstract

Remaining-useful-life (RUL) models for lithium-ion cells almost always read one input: the cycling curve. Pulse relaxation and impedance spectroscopy probe the same cell from a different physical angle and are routinely collected in production and service, yet how much they add has not been quantified under a controlled protocol. We answer with a controlled comparison in which the model is held fixed and only the input channel changes. On 64 LFP cells spanning 11 operating conditions, adding the two-RC parameters of a 50 ms-to-100 s pulse relaxation to a discharge-curve baseline reduces cell-macro error from 6.587 to 6.020 remaining-inspection units (8.6%, 5000-resample cell-level bootstrap 95% CI [-0.999, -0.133]), with the same direction under leave-one-cell-out (5.069 to 4.591) and 62.5% of cells improved. Fitted physical parameters still outperform unfitted waveform descriptors once the descriptor count is nearly matched (6.020 at 30 dimensions vs 6.109 at 28), though the margin narrows from 0.135 to 0.089, so roughly a third to a half of the apparent parameterisation advantage is a dimension effect. We then try to explain the gain away. A trajectory encoder with test-time self-supervision (7.825), its causal variant (7.452), hand-crafted trajectory descriptors (6.805) and a causal physical regulariser (6.274) all lose to the fixed-model baseline; the only better number, 5.724, comes from a transductive isotonic projection that reads the whole query batch and is therefore not deployable. Stronger representation learning does not help, which locates the gain in the channel rather than in fitting capacity. Finally we test whether the parameter family survives a change of operating condition on an independent 48-cell dataset covering 23 conditions: after Bonferroni correction only the fast polarisation amplitude and the steady-state voltage keep a consistent direction (19/23 and 18/23, p = 0.0013 and 0.0053); both time constants, the slow polarisation amplitude and the total excursion all fail. We report the boundary rather than smoothing it over.

## 1 Introduction

RUL prediction is the input to safety warnings, maintenance scheduling and second-life valuation. Almost all published models read the same signal: the cycling curve. Capacity fade trajectories, discharge voltage curves and incremental-capacity peaks are all products of the charge-discharge process itself. The cell also offers a second family of observations, however: constant-current pulse relaxation, long rest relaxation and electrochemical impedance. These measure polarisation dynamics and internal resistance, and in principle carry information that cycling does not.

The literature uses such measurements, but as feature engineering rather than as a question. Incremental-capacity studies show that the first peak tracks state of health and survives changes of charge rate and temperature when fine-tuned; dQ/dV analysis attributes curve changes to degradation modes. A recent critical review lists impedance, relaxation-time distribution and relaxation-voltage descriptors among the observable channels and argues that estimator choice should follow observability rather than benchmark convenience. Yet none of this work performs the comparison that would settle the matter: the same model, the same queries, the same protocol, with and without the non-cyclic channel.

This paper performs that comparison. We hold the model fixed, vary only the feature set, and evaluate under leave-one-condition-out (LOGO) and leave-one-cell-out (LOCO). On 64 LFP cells across 11 conditions, adding two-RC parameters fitted to pulse relaxation reduces cell-macro error from 6.587 to 6.020 (8.6%, CI [-0.999, -0.133]); dimension-matched waveform descriptors give 6.109 (7.3%), so most but not all of the gain is attributable to the fitted parameters rather than to feature count.

A reviewer should object that the gain comes from stronger representation learning rather than from the measurement. We test that objection directly and it fails. Four progressively stronger representation schemes, including a trajectory encoder with test-time self-supervision and two physical regularisers, all produce worse error than the fixed-model baseline. If fitting capacity were the source of the gain, these should have won.

The boundary is stated rather than hidden. All prediction results come from one dataset. On an independent 48-cell dataset spanning 23 operating conditions, only two of six two-RC parameters keep a consistent degradation direction after multiple-comparison correction; both time constants, the slow polarisation amplitude and the total excursion flip sign across conditions. A transductive projection that would improve the headline number is identified and excluded as non-deployable.

Contributions:
- A controlled channel comparison quantifying the incremental value of non-cyclic measurements for cross-condition RUL (8.6% LOGO, CI [-0.999, -0.133], LOCO concordant, 62.5% of cells improved).
- A ladder of stronger representation-learning baselines that rules out the "better modelling" explanation; all of them lose to the fixed model.
- An explicit applicability boundary obtained on a second dataset with a pre-registered criterion and condition-level statistical units, showing which parameters survive a change of operating condition and which do not.

## 2 Related Work

**Cycling-curve features.** The dominant input is the cycling curve. Incremental capacity and differential voltage analysis turn the voltage-capacity relation into curves with distinguishable peaks, and these peaks are used as health features. The generation of such curves is not standardised: a review across three public datasets finds no agreed procedure and recommends point counting with a Savitzky-Golay filter. The same literature attributes dQ/dV shape changes to specific degradation modes, which is what motivates treating these curves as degradation state rather than as operating history.

**Non-cyclic measurements.** Pulse and impedance measurements are established for state-of-health estimation. A unified evaluation of incremental-capacity features reports that the horizontal and vertical coordinates of the first peak suffice for accurate SOH estimation from partial charging data (MAPE 4.82% on CALCE, 0.65% on TJU), with fine-tuning transferring across charge rate and temperature. This establishes a stable measurement-to-degradation mapping; it does not establish an increment over cycling data for RUL.

**Cross-condition transfer.** Three routes exist. Input-side normalisation removes condition-dependent components but risks removing degradation signal. Label-side relative learning predicts a lifetime difference between pairs of cells, which cancels condition-common terms. Model-side approaches use gated mixtures of experts or test-time adaptation, the latter adapting continuously on unlabeled data as it arrives and using a one-RC equivalent-circuit equation as a self-supervised constraint.

**Gap.** The measurement channel is known to be informative for SOH, and the channel is recognised in review frameworks, but the incremental value for RUL under a cross-condition protocol has not been quantified, and the alternative explanation - that a richer representation simply fits better - has not been excluded.

## 3 Data and Protocol

**Primary dataset.** UConn-ISU-ILCC LFP cells: 64 cells in 11 operating-condition groups. Each cell is characterised at up to 77 reference performance tests, each providing a slow pulse at 20/50/90% SOC in both directions (101-point relaxation trace) and a constant-current discharge curve.

**Timeline.** The dataset's `num_cycles` field is not monotone in time: sorting by it produces 379 increases in state of health across cells, against 16 when sorting by inspection index. We therefore use the inspection index as the time axis and express RUL as the number of remaining inspections to the first observation with SOH <= 80%. This removes all negative RUL values (0 of 2,813 rows).

**Secondary dataset.** Bayreuth 2025: 48 cells, 23 valid condition combinations (temperature {0,10,25,30,45,55} C x C-rate {0.5,0.75,1.0,1.5}), each cell providing 72 long rests of about three hours. Used only for the applicability-boundary test; its cells fade by 1.0-10.4% according to the dataset metadata (0.97-12.49% over the parsed 74-cycle windows, the difference being the reference-capacity definition) and never approach 80% SOH, so it cannot define an RUL target.

**Excluded datasets.** NASA PCoE: of 38 files only 4 cells have a complete lifetime trajectory. CALCE: CS2/CX2/A123 provide full lifetimes but no impedance over life, the impedance archives being a separate characterisation experiment.

## 4 Method

**Physical parameterisation.** For each pulse trace we fit a two-RC relaxation model to the post-peak segment,

`v(t) = c + a1 exp(-t/tau1) + a2 exp(-t/tau2)`

yielding a steady-state voltage `c`, two polarisation amplitudes `a1, a2` and two time constants. Six traces per inspection (3 SOC x 2 directions) give 30 parameters. All 17,130 traces converged (0% NaN, 49 s on one CPU core).

**Feature sets.** A_curve: 50-bin means of the discharge curve plus mean, standard deviation, minimum and maximum (54 dimensions). A_prefix: A_curve plus capacity and SOH (58). C_desc20: waveform descriptors - voltage excursion, recovery fraction, 63%-recovery time (20). C_desc30: the same 20 plus the initial post-peak slope of each of the six traces and two cross-SOC spread terms (28), used as a dimension-matched control against C_ecm. C_ecm: the 30 fitted parameters. Combinations are formed by concatenation.

**Model and protocols.** One `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` (300 iterations, learning rate 0.08) is used for every feature set; nothing else changes. LOGO holds out one condition group at a time (11 folds); LOCO holds out one cell (64 folds). The metric is cell-macro mean absolute error: absolute error is averaged within each cell, then across cells, so that cells with many queries do not dominate.

**Statistics.** 5,000-resample cell-level paired bootstrap for the difference against A_curve.

## 5 Results

**5.1 Channel increment.** Adding pulse parameters to the discharge curve reduces LOGO error from 6.587 to 6.020 (8.6%, CI [-0.999, -0.133]) and LOCO error from 5.069 to 4.591; 62.5% of cells improve. Unfitted descriptors at 20 dimensions reach 6.155 (6.6%) and at 28 dimensions 6.109 (7.3%). Dimension matching therefore shrinks the fitted-parameter advantage from 0.135 to 0.089 (1.4%), so roughly a third to a half of the apparent parameterisation benefit is a dimension effect. Pulse parameters alone are weak (7.789), so the channel complements the cycling curve rather than replacing it.

**5.2 Ruling out representation learning.** We built four progressively stronger schemes. A GRU over the last 16 inspections of pulse parameters, pre-trained with RUL regression plus masked reconstruction and adapted at test time with reconstruction only, reaches 7.825; the causal variant, adapting only on each cell's first half, reaches 7.452. Hand-crafted trajectory descriptors (first value, current value, slope, range per parameter) reach 6.805. A causal physical regulariser enforcing monotone RUL against the inspection index reaches 6.274. Every scheme loses to the fixed-model baseline. The one better number, 5.724, comes from a per-cell isotonic projection fitted on the whole query batch; it reads future queries and is reported only as an upper bound.

**5.3 Applicability boundary.** On Bayreuth, 45 of 48 cells contributed valid relaxations across 23 conditions. For each parameter we compute the median Spearman correlation with SOH within each condition, then count how many conditions share the majority sign, with a one-sided binomial test and Bonferroni correction over six parameters (alpha = 0.0083). Fast polarisation amplitude passes (19/23, p = 0.0013) as does steady-state voltage (18/23, p = 0.0053). Four parameters fail: both time constants (17/23, p = 0.017 for the fast one; 14/23, p = 0.202 for the slow one), the slow polarisation amplitude (13/23, p = 0.339) and the total excursion (17/23, p = 0.017). Within-condition monotonicity is nevertheless strong for every parameter (median |rho| 0.59-0.81), so the failure is one of direction, not of signal.

**5.4 Why the unit of analysis matters.** Each Bayreuth condition holds one or two cells, and cells within a condition are strongly correlated. Using cells as the unit would nearly double n and would flip the 0.74 parameters from non-significant to significant. We pre-registered the condition as the unit and report it that way.

## 6 Limitations

1. Prediction results come from a single dataset of 64 cells. The second dataset supports only the boundary analysis.
2. The headline interval is narrow: the bound closest to zero is the upper one, -0.133, so the effect is significant but not large.
3. Four of six physical parameters fail the cross-condition test, including one of the two amplitudes; only the fast polarisation amplitude and the steady-state voltage survive, and the claim is restricted to those two.
4. Pulse measurements are available in the primary dataset at reference performance tests; continuous in-field pulse collection is assumed rather than demonstrated.
5. The stratified analysis by degradation stage and SOC coverage (E3) has not been run and is not claimed.

## 7 Conclusion

Non-cyclic measurements carry information that cycling curves do not, and the increment survives a cross-condition protocol and a ladder of stronger representation-learning baselines. It is not large - 8.6% with an interval that only just excludes zero - and it does not extend to every parameter of the physical model: only the fast polarisation amplitude and the steady-state voltage transfer across operating conditions, while both time constants, the slow amplitude and the total excursion do not. We report both.
